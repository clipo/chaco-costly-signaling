## Theme 1: Costly Signaling Theory and Monumental Architecture

Foundational and applied theory on costly signaling, the handicap principle, and archaeological applications to monumental construction. These sources provide the theoretical scaffolding for the three-layer model.

---

### Zahavi, A. (1975). Mate selection: A selection for a handicap.

**Citation:** Zahavi, A. 1975. Mate selection: A selection for a handicap. *Journal of Theoretical Biology* 53(1):205-214. DOI: 10.1016/0022-5193(75)90111-3
**Access level:** Citation only
**Relevance:** High — foundational theoretical source; the handicap principle is directly encoded in the model's Layer 1
**Theme:** Costly signaling theory
**Evidence type:** Theoretical
**[UNREAD: known by citation only, not yet accessed]**

**Annotation:** Proposed that elaborate traits impose survival handicaps, functioning to demonstrate genetic quality. The biological analog to Spence's signaling condition: signal honesty is maintained because cost is differentially borne by quality types.

**Citing context:** Referenced in virtually all costly signaling literature; the model formally encodes the Zahavi condition through the cost function c(x,q) = x^2/(2q).
**Why included:** Foundational theoretical source for Layer 1 of the model.

### Spence, M. (1973). Job Market Signaling.

**Citation:** Spence, M. 1973. Job Market Signaling. *Quarterly Journal of Economics* 87(3):355-374. DOI: 10.2307/1882010
**Access level:** Citation only
**Relevance:** High — the Spence condition is the core mechanism in Layer 1
**Theme:** Costly signaling theory
**Evidence type:** Theoretical
**[UNREAD: known by citation only, not yet accessed]**

**Annotation:** Established the separating equilibrium in signaling games where signal cost is negatively correlated with sender quality. The "Spence condition" (cross-partial derivative of cost with respect to signal and quality is negative) is the formal criterion ensuring signal honesty.

**Citing context:** The three-layer model names this condition explicitly; the cost function c(x,q) = x^2/(2q) satisfies it because higher-quality agents face lower marginal costs of investment.
**Why included:** Direct theoretical foundation for the model's individual signaling equilibrium.

### Hawkes, K. & Bliege Bird, R. (2002). Showing off, handicap signaling, and the evolution of men's work.

**Citation:** Hawkes, K. & Bliege Bird, R. 2002. Showing off, handicap signaling, and the evolution of men's work. *Evolutionary Anthropology* 11(2):58-67. DOI: 10.1002/evan.20005
**Access level:** Abstract only
**Relevance:** High — connects handicap principle to human cooperative behaviors and the "waste" concept
**Theme:** Costly signaling theory
**Evidence type:** Theoretical/Review
**[PARTIAL: annotation based on abstract/metadata only]**

**Annotation:** Reviews the handicap principle's application to human behavioral ecology, arguing that "waste" is a common outcome of signal selection because the cost of signal reveals the show-off's hidden qualities. Connects Zahavi's framework to ethnographic patterns of male provisioning and public generosity.

**What's known:** Central argument that apparently wasteful male labor (big-game hunting, public generosity) functions as costly signaling. Synthesis of signaling theory with human behavioral ecology.
**What's unknown:** Specific formal conditions distinguishing waste-as-signal from inefficiency; how the framework handles group-level (vs. individual) signaling.

### Bliege Bird, R. & Smith, E.A. (2005). Signaling theory, strategic interaction, and symbolic capital.

**Access level:** Abstract only
**Relevance:** High — major review bridging signaling theory and anthropological applications
**Theme:** Costly signaling theory
**Evidence type:** Review
**[PARTIAL: annotation based on abstract/metadata only]**

**Annotation:** Defines criteria for costly signals in human contexts: differentially costly, honestly linked to quality, broadcast to multiple observers. ### Guiterman, C.H. et al. (2015). Eleventh-century shift in timber procurement areas for the great houses of Chaco Canyon.

**Citation:** Guiterman, C.H., Swetnam, T.W. & Dean, J.S. 2015. Eleventh-century shift in timber procurement areas for the great houses of Chaco Canyon, NM. *PNAS* 112(44):13438-13443. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.1514272112
**Access level:** Abstract only
**Relevance:** High — quantifies timber transport distances and procurement shifts
**Theme:** Construction costs and labor
**Evidence type:** Empirical
**[PARTIAL: annotation based on abstract/metadata only]**

**Annotation:** Used tree-ring sourcing to demonstrate that 70% of the 240,000+ timbers used in great house construction originated over 75 km from Chaco Canyon. **What's unknown:** Total labor budgets for timber procurement; whether the shift reflects changing exchange relationships or resource depletion.
