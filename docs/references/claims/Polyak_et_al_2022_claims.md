## Claims

### Claim 1

**Statement**: The puebloan Late Holocene climatic interval (1260 to 370 yr B2K, i.e., ca. AD 740 to 1630) was the most arid and highly variable climatic period of the last 3400 years, as recorded by stalagmite HC-1 from Hidden Cave in the Guadalupe Mountains.
**Page**: p. 1 (abstract)
**Type**: empirical
**Evidence cited by author**: Stalagmite HC-1 growth record, mineral assemblage (calcite/aragonite transitions), stable isotope time-series (delta-18O, delta-13C)
**Depends on**: Validity of calcite-to-aragonite transition as aridity proxy; U-series chronology accuracy
**Notes**: The record covers 3400 years. "Puebloan Late Holocene" is their term for the interval coeval with Pueblo cultural stages I-IV.
**Evidence strength**: Strong

### Claim 2

**Statement**: Climatic conditions favoring the introduction of cultivation existed prior to the Pueblo period, during more pluvial-like conditions from at least 3400 to 1260 yr B2K (ca. 1400 BC to AD 740).
**Page**: p. 1 (abstract)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: Stalagmite HC-1 continuous calcite growth with low delta-18O and delta-13C values during this interval, indicating near-equilibrium precipitation under wetter conditions
**Depends on**: Claim 1
**Notes**: This "pluvial Late Holocene" interval is characterized by faster, more uniform calcite growth with only two brief hiatuses.
**Evidence strength**: Strong

### Claim 3

**Statement**: The change from the Desert Archaic/Basketmaker to Pueblo cultures was associated with a quick transition to increasing aridity into and through the Pueblo period, associated with greater urbanization and the establishment of pueblo population centers.
**Page**: p. 1 (abstract)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: Temporal coincidence between aragonite layers/growth hiatuses in HC-1 and Pueblo cultural stages
**Depends on**: Claims 1 and 2; correlation between regional climate proxy and cultural change
**Notes**: The authors propose that pueblo development was a coping strategy for increasing aridity, a major interpretive claim.
**Evidence strength**: Moderate

### Claim 4

**Statement**: Stalagmite HC-1 growth from approximately 3320 to 1260 yr B2K represents the thickest sequence of uninterrupted growth, with an average rate of 0.085 mm/yr and no distinct aragonite layers or hiatuses, indicating pluvial conditions coeval with Basketmaker II and III cultures.
**Page**: p. 3
**Type**: empirical
**Evidence cited by author**: 34 U-series dates; grayscale imaging; growth rate measurements
**Depends on**: U-series chronology accuracy
**Notes**: This 2000-year interval of continuous calcite growth is the strongest evidence for a prolonged wet period preceding the Pueblo era.
**Evidence strength**: Strong

### Claim 5

**Statement**: Growth from 1258 to 366 yr B2K was markedly slower (average 0.015 mm/yr) and interrupted, with two 1-2 mm thick aragonite layers, several thinner hiatuses, and a notable grayscale change at 1416 yr B2K, collectively defining the puebloan Late Holocene as climatically distinct.
**Page**: p. 3
**Type**: empirical
**Evidence cited by author**: U-series dates; aragonite layer identification; growth rate calculations
**Depends on**: U-series chronology; mineral assemblage identification
**Notes**: The growth rate dropped nearly six-fold compared to the pluvial interval, indicating substantially drier conditions.
**Evidence strength**: Strong

### Claim 6

**Statement**: The correlation of delta-18O and delta-13C in stalagmite HC-1 is strong (R = 0.82, p < 0.00001), driven by kinetic fractionation associated with evaporation and rapid CO2 degassing. Higher values indicate drier, more evaporative climate; lower values indicate more humid conditions near isotopic equilibrium.
**Page**: pp. 3-4
**Type**: empirical / methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: 553 stable isotope analyses; comparison with predicted isotopic equilibrium values; drip water delta-18O data from Carlsbad Cavern
**Depends on**: Interpretation of covariation as reflecting climate rather than other cave processes
**Notes**: The strong covariation provides independent support for the mineral assemblage proxy. The puebloan sub-period samples cluster toward the arid/kinetic end of the trend line (Figure 4).
**Evidence strength**: Strong

### Claim 7

**Statement**: Pueblo I (starting at 1300 yr B2K) marks the onset of drier conditions with the first aragonite-defined drought at approximately 1258 yr B2K. Between sets of droughts at the beginning and end of Pueblo I, approximately 50 years of average calcite growth indicates pluvial-like climate in the middle of Pueblo I.
**Page**: p. 6
**Type**: empirical
**Evidence cited by author**: Aragonite layers in HC-1; grayscale time-series; PDSI comparison (Figure 5)
**Depends on**: U-series chronology; aragonite-drought interpretation
**Notes**: Five aragonite layers (droughts) occur within a span of 30-40 years starting at 1180 yr B2K (ca. AD 820).
**Evidence strength**: Strong

### Claim 8

**Statement**: Pueblo II in the stalagmite HC-1 record is a 255-year interval of more frequent aragonite-defined droughts (ca. AD 860-1140). The age model places an approximately 90-year growth hiatus or period of very slow growth after 950 yr B2K (ca. AD 1050), indicating severe drought.
**Page**: p. 6
**Type**: empirical
**Evidence cited by author**: Aragonite layers; growth hiatus; HC-1 chronology
**Depends on**: U-series chronology
**Notes**: Pueblo II would have been much drier than Pueblo I. The approximately 90-year growth hiatus around AD 1050 is notable because it coincides with peak Chaco florescence.
**Evidence strength**: Strong

### Claim 9

**Statement**: Pueblo III in the stalagmite HC-1 record spans approximately 140 years with numerous but less severe aragonite-defined droughts compared to Pueblo II, creating an approximately 140-year interval of "predictable" climate variability, with evenly spaced droughts that would have been possibly easier for cultures to adapt to.
**Page**: pp. 6-7
**Type**: empirical / interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: Aragonite layer count and spacing; grayscale time-series (Figure 6C)
**Depends on**: U-series chronology
**Notes**: The characterization of Pueblo III climate as "predictable" is an important interpretive addition to the empirical observation of evenly spaced aragonite layers.
**Evidence strength**: Moderate

### Claim 10

**Statement**: Pueblo IV (from 685 to 560 yr B2K, ca. AD 1315-1440) was the driest interval of the puebloan Late Holocene, represented by slower stalagmite growth, thick aragonite layers, and the longest growth hiatus in the 3400-year record (approximately 210 years, from 560 to 370 yr B2K), interpreted as a "super drought."
**Page**: p. 7
**Type**: empirical
**Evidence cited by author**: Growth hiatus duration; aragonite layer thickness; comparison with Asmerom et al. (2013) super drought designation
**Depends on**: U-series chronology
**Notes**: The super drought corresponds to the period when Spanish explorers arrived in the SWUS (ca. AD 1540), finding ancestral American populations clustered along permanent water ways.
**Evidence strength**: Strong

### Claim 11

**Statement**: The puebloan Late Holocene sub-period was coeval with cool dry climatic conditions on land from approximately 1550 to 700 yr B2K, linked to cool sea surface temperatures along the California coast and a time of increased sociopolitical complexity.
**Page**: p. 8
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: Comparison with other paleoclimate records; Kennett and Kennett (2000) on coastal California
**Depends on**: Synchroneity of multiple regional records
**Notes**: This links the Pueblo period aridity to a broader Pacific-driven climate pattern, consistent with the ENSO framework in Gillreath-Brown et al. 2024.
**Evidence strength**: Moderate

### Claim 12

**Statement**: A study of seven SW USA cultural traditions from 1100 to 500 yr B2K could not show a statistically significant robust relationship between drought and dramatic cultural change. The stalagmite HC-1 record differs because it contextualizes the entire pre-Hispanic Pueblo period as the climatically driest part of the Late Holocene.
**Page**: p. 8
**Type**: methodological / interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: Kintigh, K. W. & Ingram, S. E. (2018), reference 8; HC-1 record comparison
**Depends on**: HC-1 record validity; characterization of Kintigh & Ingram results
**Notes**: The authors argue that the failure to find drought-culture correlations at the level of individual megadroughts may be because the relevant climate signal is the long-term baseline shift, not individual drought events.
**Evidence strength**: Moderate

### Claim 13

**Statement**: The authors suggest that pueblo cultural development was a way of coping with overall drier and more variable climatic conditions that extended through the entire pre-Hispanic Pueblo period, forcing ancestral Americans to develop alternative strategies including greater urbanization and integration.
**Page**: p. 9 (Conclusion)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: HC-1 record; contrast with pluvial pre-Pueblo interval
**Depends on**: All empirical claims above; assumption that cultural change was climate-driven
**Notes**: This is the paper's central interpretive claim. It reframes pueblo development as an adaptation to long-term aridification rather than a response to specific drought events.
**Evidence strength**: Moderate

## Summary Assessment

Polyak et al. 2022 provides a 3400-year high-resolution paleoclimate record from a speleothem in southeastern New Mexico that contextualizes the entire Pueblo period as occurring within the driest and most variable climatic interval of the Late Holocene. This long-term perspective complements shorter-term tree-ring-based drought records by showing that the Pueblo cultural trajectory unfolded against a backdrop of progressive aridification relative to the preceding two millennia. For the costly signaling model, the key finding is that the Chaco florescence occurred during a period of high climatic variability (Pueblo II), with the environmental uncertainty parameter (sigma) elevated well above the pre-Pueblo baseline. The approximately 90-year growth hiatus around AD 1050, coinciding with peak Chaco construction, is consistent with the prediction that signaling investment intensifies during environmental stress.
