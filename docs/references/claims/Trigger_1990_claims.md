## Claims

### Claim 1

**Statement**: "The basic way [human beings] symbolize power is through the conspicuous consumption of energy, control of which is the fundamental measure of power."
**Page**: p. 119 (abstract)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: [Cannot verify from abstract alone]
**Depends on**: Thermodynamic/energy framework for understanding symbolic behavior
**Notes**: This is Trigger's central thesis. Energy expenditure, not information content or aesthetic quality, is the fundamental currency of power symbolism. This framing anticipates the costly signaling interpretation: the cost (energy expenditure) is the signal, not an incidental property of the behavior.

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (assertion based on cross-cultural comparison, details not available)
**Assessment**: The energy-as-power-symbol thesis is influential (281 CrossRef citations). It provides a materialist foundation for the signaling interpretation of monument construction that complements the formal game-theoretic approach.

### Claim 2

**Statement**: "Conspicuous consumption occurs in the form of monumental construction, supporting large numbers of energy consumers, production of high energy-consuming luxury goods, and an emphasis on non-useful movement (processions, needlessly large rooms, etc.)."
**Page**: p. 119 (abstract)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: [Cannot verify from abstract alone; likely draws on Veblen's conspicuous consumption and cross-cultural architectural evidence]
**Depends on**: Claim 1 (energy as power symbol)
**Notes**: Trigger identifies four channels of conspicuous energy consumption. All four are present at Chaco Canyon: monumental great houses, large resident/pilgrim populations, exotic goods (turquoise, macaws, cacao), and oversized rooms/kivas with ceremonial movement. The "non-useful movement" category (processions, needlessly large rooms) maps onto the Barclay et al. 2021 finding that signals need not benefit receivers (b = 0) to be effective.

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (cross-cultural pattern claim; details not available from abstract)
**Assessment**: The four-channel framework is analytically useful. The identification of "non-useful" features as power symbols is important for distinguishing signaling from functional explanations of architecture.

### Claim 3

**Statement**: By expanding the concept of energy-use to "cover conspicuous consumption as well as efficiency of production, it can be seen as a basic factor in shaping the political as well as the economic behaviour of human beings."
**Page**: p. 119 (abstract, continued on p. 132 per page range)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: [Cannot verify from abstract alone]
**Depends on**: Claims 1 and 2
**Notes**: Trigger argues for a unified energy framework that encompasses both production (economic behavior, optimization) and consumption (political behavior, signaling). This parallels the mls-monuments model's integration of economic costs (C = 0.35 productivity diversion) with political benefits (conflict deterrence, cooperation network formation).

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (theoretical claim; degree of empirical support not assessable from abstract)
**Assessment**: The unification of production and consumption under an energy framework is conceptually valuable but risks being tautological without specific predictions.

### Claim 4

**Statement**: The energy framework "can explain why, as systems of inequality evolve, monumental architecture becomes an increasingly prominent feature of the archaeological record."
**Page**: p. 119 (abstract)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: [Cannot verify from abstract alone; presumably based on cross-cultural comparison of architectural scale and social complexity]
**Depends on**: Claims 1-3
**Notes**: This is a directional prediction: increasing inequality should correlate with increasing monument scale. For Chaco, this predicts that the escalation of great house construction (850-1100 CE) should track increasing social differentiation, which is broadly consistent with the archaeological evidence (elite burials, restricted exchange networks).

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (the cross-cultural correlation is well-documented in the literature, though Trigger's specific evidence cannot be verified from the abstract)
**Assessment**: The prediction is qualitatively correct but lacks formal specification of the inequality-monument relationship. The mls-monuments model provides a more precise mechanism through the Spence condition.

### Claim 5

**Statement**: The enlarged energy concept "would also broaden a materialist perspective on human behaviour to take account of many significant aspects of the ideational components of such behaviour that appear in the archaeological record."
**Page**: pp. 119-132 (abstract/conclusion)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: [Cannot verify from abstract alone]
**Depends on**: Claims 1-4
**Notes**: Trigger argues that the energy framework bridges the processual-postprocessual divide by incorporating symbolic/ideational behavior (typically claimed by postprocessualists) within a materialist energy-expenditure framework. This methodological position aligns with the signaling approach, which explains symbolic behavior as adaptive and materially grounded.

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (methodological argument)
**Assessment**: The bridge between materialist and ideational approaches is a significant methodological contribution. The signaling framework achieves a similar integration by treating symbolic investment as strategic behavior with measurable costs and benefits.

### Claim 7

**Statement**: [CITE-CHECK: Trigger 1990] Human beings cope with production and distribution of goods by trying to achieve maximum efficiencies in energy expenditures (the economic domain), but symbolize power through the opposite, conspicuous waste of energy (the political domain).
**Page**: p. 119 (paraphrase from abstract)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: [Cannot verify from abstract alone]
**Depends on**: Distinction between economic optimization and political display
**Notes**: The tension between efficiency and display is directly relevant to the mls-monuments model's cost parameter. The C = 0.35 represents the "waste" that is diverted from productive activity to signaling. Trigger's framework explains why this waste is not maladaptive: it serves a political function (power symbolism) that cannot be achieved through efficient production alone.

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (well-argued in abstract; full development not available)
**Assessment**: The efficiency-display tension is a useful framing. The signaling model provides a formal explanation for why display (waste) can be individually rational despite its apparent inefficiency.

### Claim 8

**Statement**: [CITE-CHECK: Trigger 1990] The concept of thermodynamics (energy transformation and expenditure) provides a unifying framework for understanding both the economic and symbolic dimensions of monumental architecture.
**Page**: Title and abstract
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: [Cannot verify from abstract alone]
**Depends on**: Physics/thermodynamics analogy applied to cultural behavior
**Notes**: The "thermodynamic" framing in the title is metaphorical rather than formal. Trigger uses energy expenditure as a cross-culturally commensurable measure, not as a physical model of entropy or work. This is methodologically important: it provides a common metric (energy cost) for comparing monuments across cultures, similar to how the mls-monuments model uses labor diversion (C) as a standardized cost parameter.

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (methodological contribution)
**Assessment**: The energy metric is useful for cross-cultural comparison but has limitations. Energy expenditure does not capture all relevant dimensions of signaling cost (e.g., opportunity costs, risk, social capital). The mls-monuments model's C parameter is more encompassing.

## Claim Type Definitions
