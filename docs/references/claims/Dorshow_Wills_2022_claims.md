## Claims

### Claim 1

**Statement**: "An agricultural suitability study for a 3,923 km2 area encompassing Chaco Canyon and the surrounding San Juan Basin in northwest New Mexico is presented with implications for farming during the great house period, ca. AD 850 to 1150 (Bonito Phase)."

**Page**: Abstract

**Type**: methodological

**Evidence cited by author**: GIS-based analysis

**Depends on**: GIS methodology and variable selection

**Notes**: The study area (3,923 km2) is substantially larger than previous studies of agricultural potential in the Chaco region.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (methodological claim about scope)

**Assessment**: The expanded study area is important for evaluating the dispersed farming model proposed by Wills 2017.

### Claim 3

**Statement**: "The results indicate that the largest great houses and surrounding communities were located in extensive areas of highly suitable agricultural lands."

**Page**: Abstract

**Type**: empirical

**Evidence cited by author**: GIS suitability analysis results

**Depends on**: Suitability classification criteria; great house location data

**Notes**: This directly supports the argument in Wills 2017 that great house placement was related to agricultural productive capacity of surrounding lands.

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (cannot evaluate without seeing the analysis)

**Assessment**: The finding that great houses correlate with agricultural suitability is expected but important for establishing that the political economy was embedded in productive landscapes.

## Summary Assessment

Dorshow & Wills 2022 appears to be a significant GIS-based study that expands the agricultural suitability analysis of the Chaco Canyon region to a 3,923 km2 area. The central finding, that great houses are located in zones of high agricultural suitability, supports the labor-recruitment political economy model developed in Wills 2017. However, because only the abstract is available in the repository PDF, none of the specific empirical results, methods, or figures can be evaluated. The full paper is needed to extract parameter values relevant to the costly signaling model (e.g., estimated carrying capacity per drainage, productivity estimates, spatial distribution of suitable lands relative to great house locations).
