### Claim 2

**Statement**: Four criteria must be met for a behavior to qualify as a costly signal: (1) within-group variance in an unobservable attribute exists, (2) observers benefit from reliable information about that attribute, (3) higher-quality signalers benefit from broadcasting but deception is possible, (4) the cost/benefit ratio is correlated with signaler quality.
**Page**: Not directly visible; reported in Quinn 2019 (p. 277) and Kantner & Vaughn 2012 (p. 70) as deriving from this paper and Boone 1998, Smith & Bliege Bird 2003
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: [Reported via Quinn 2019 and Kantner & Vaughn 2012]
**Depends on**: Formal signaling theory
**Notes**: These four criteria are the standard operationalization of CST used throughout the archaeological literature. They correspond to the Spence condition and handicap principle in the project's formal model. [UNVERIFIED: page number from original paper not confirmed]
**Evidence strength**: Weak (derived from secondary sources; needs verification from full paper)

## Summary Assessment

Bliege Bird & Smith 2005 is widely cited as a key theoretical synthesis linking costly signaling theory to anthropological questions about generosity, prestige goods, and symbolic capital. Based on secondary characterizations in Quinn 2019, Hawkes & Bliege Bird 2002, and Kantner & Vaughn 2012, it establishes the standard four-criteria framework for evaluating costly signals and provides empirical grounding from Meriam Islander ethnography. However, the PDF in the repository is a 1-page browser screenshot, not the actual paper. The full text must be obtained before any claims from this paper can be used in manuscript prose.
