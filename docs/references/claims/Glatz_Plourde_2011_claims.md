## Summary Assessment

Glatz & Plourde 2011 applies costly signaling theory to landscape monuments of the Late Bronze Age Hittite Empire, arguing that these monuments moderated ongoing territorial contests rather than merely commemorating hegemony. The paper derives spatial and temporal predictions from CST and tests them against the Hittite archaeological record. This is one of the few archaeological studies that moves beyond verbal application of CST to generate specific testable expectations about monument distribution. The key relevance to the Chaco project is threefold: (1) the boundary-placement prediction parallels predictions about outlier great house locations, (2) the temporal intensification-during-competition prediction parallels the lambda(sigma) prediction, and (3) the state-level application demonstrates CST's scalability.
