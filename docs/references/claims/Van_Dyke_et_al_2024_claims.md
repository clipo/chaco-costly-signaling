# Claims Extraction: Van Dyke et al. 2024

**Source:** Van Dyke, Ruth M., K.E. Primeau, Kellam Throgmorton, and David E. Witt. 2024. Seashells and Sound Waves: Modelling Soundscapes in Chacoan Great-House Communities. *Antiquity* 98(399):777-794.

**DOI:** 10.15184/aqy.2024.54

---

## Claim 1

- **Statement:** The modelled reach of a conch-shell trumpet blast from a Chacoan great house consistently maps onto the distribution of associated habitation sites across five communities studied.
- **Page:** 777 (abstract), 788
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** GIS-based soundshed analysis using the Soundshed Analysis Tool v.0.9.3 (Primeau 2022)
- **Depends on:** Sound propagation modelling; lidar elevation data; habitation site location data
- **Notes:** Five communities tested: Bis sa'ani, Kin Klizhin, Morris 40, Padilla Wash, Pierre's. Statistical tests confirm patterns are unlikely due to chance.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; consistent pattern across five independent communities with statistical validation.
- **Assessment:** Novel methodological contribution. The consistency across five communities strengthens the finding.

---

## Claim 2

- **Statement:** Conch-shell trumpets (Strombus sp. and Murex sp.) had to be procured from the Pacific Ocean, approximately 1000 km to the south-west, and are used in contemporary Hopi and Zuni Pueblo ritual practices. A 200mm-long shell used by Loose (2012) produced a 96dB sound at 329.84Hz, with harmonic overtones at 650Hz and 974.4Hz.
- **Page:** 779-780
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Brown 2005:291-305; Vokes and Gregory 2007; Mills and Ferguson 2008; Loose 2012
- **Depends on:** Acoustic experiments and ethnographic records
- **Notes:** The 1000 km procurement distance makes conch shells a long-distance exotic good. The 96dB sound pressure level is the basis for the modelling.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong for acoustic measurements (experimental replication); strong for ethnographic use; moderate for procurement distance (estimated).
- **Assessment:** The long-distance procurement of conch shells places them in the same category as turquoise, macaws, and cacao as costly imported exotica.

## Claim 3

- **Statement:** Most of the 46 known conch shells or fragments from the ancient Southwest were recovered from areas nearer the ocean, but excavators found 17 in Chaco Canyon. A conch-shell trumpet was found beneath a plank floor in Room 33 of Pueblo Bonito, associated with Chaco Canyon's most elaborate burial.
- **Page:** 780
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Brown 2005:299-300; Mills and Ferguson 2008:347, tab. 1
- **Depends on:** Excavation records
- **Notes:** 17 of 46 known Southwest conch shells concentrated at Chaco is a striking concentration (37%). Room 33 is the richest burial context at Pueblo Bonito.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; based on excavation records and museum collections.
- **Assessment:** The association with the most elaborate burial directly links conch-shell trumpets to elite status display.

## Claim 4

- **Statement:** At Kin Klizhin, a trumpet blast from the great house would have been audible at 16 of 23 (69.6%) habitation sites. Statistical tests indicate this was highly unlikely due to chance alone (p <= 0.001).
- **Page:** 784
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Soundshed modelling results; Fisher's exact test (Table S1)
- **Depends on:** BLM lidar data; site location data from Marshall et al. 1979, Powers et al. 1983
- **Notes:** Kin Klizhin is an "ancestral" community (AD 875-1150).
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; statistical test against random expectation.
- **Assessment:** One of the strongest individual results in the study.

---

## Claim 5

- **Statement:** At Pierre's community, six out of nine (66.7%) habitations were located in the audibility zone of a conch-shell blast from Great House A, and seven out of nine (77.8%) in the audibility zone from El Faro. The positioning of sites within the audibility zone was extremely unlikely to be related to chance alone (p = 0.001, <= 0.001, and <= 0.001 for three scenarios).
- **Page:** 787-788
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Soundshed modelling results; Fisher's exact test (Table S1)
- **Depends on:** BLM lidar data; site location data from Powers et al. 1983:94-122
- **Notes:** Pierre's is a "scion" community (AD 1100-1150), associated with the Great North Road.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; multiple source points tested with consistent results.
- **Assessment:** The El Faro result (77.8%) is notable because El Faro is an ERF atop a sandstone pinnacle, suggesting deliberate placement for acoustic reach.

## Claim 6

- **Statement:** On a quiet morning, the conch could be heard over ambient background sounds for a distance of up to approximately 1418m.
- **Page:** 783
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Soundshed modelling (Table 1 parameters: 96dB source at 6ft height, 330Hz, 30% humidity, 55.4F, 20.7dB ambient)
- **Depends on:** Sound propagation modelling at dawn on summer solstice
- **Notes:** The 1418m range defines the maximum community radius that could be reached by a trumpet blast.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; modelled rather than field-tested, but parameters are conservative.
- **Assessment:** The ~1.4km range is the key spatial constraint for interpreting soundshed-community overlap.

## Claim 7

- **Statement:** A chi-square test for association between five communities showed no statistically significant difference in the proportion of habitation sites within audibility zones (chi-squared = 2.4956, p = 0.645 for best-case; chi-squared = 1.1267, p = 0.890 for more stringent parameters). All five communities exhibit the same preference for placing habitation sites within audibility zones.
- **Page:** 788
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Chi-square tests (Tables S2, S3)
- **Depends on:** Soundshed results across all five communities
- **Notes:** The lack of significant difference between communities means the pattern is consistent, not site-specific.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; cross-community statistical consistency.
- **Assessment:** The consistency is the key finding: this is a system-wide phenomenon, not a local anomaly.

---

## Claim 8

- **Statement:** A slightly higher percentage of habitations are located within audibility zones in "scion" communities (Bis sa'ani and Pierre's) compared to "ancestral" communities (Kin Klizhin, Padilla Wash, Morris 40). This may reflect increasing formality in settlement locations in the later (post-1100) Chacoan world.
- **Page:** 789
- **Type:** Interpretive
- **Evidence cited by author:** Comparison of community types (Table S4); Van Dyke et al. 2016 visibility studies; Throgmorton 2019
- **Depends on:** Community chronology and type classification
- **Notes:** The ancestral/scion distinction is important: ancestral communities predate Chaco expansion; scion communities were founded during expansion.
- **Evidence strength:** Weak; small sample sizes and the difference is not statistically significant.
- **Assessment:** Suggestive but not conclusive. The pattern is consistent with increasing organizational formality over time.

## Claim 9

- **Statement:** The acoustic reach of a conch-shell trumpet could have been one way of ensuring the internal cohesion of Chacoan communities. "If leaders atop great houses needed to quickly communicate with all community residents, a conch-shell blast would have been a more effective method than relying on community residents to look in the right direction at the right time to see, for example, smoke/mirror signals."
- **Page:** 790
- **Type:** Interpretive
- **Evidence cited by author:** Comparison with intervisibility studies (Van Dyke et al. 2016); Figure 10 showing soundshed-viewshed overlap at Padilla Wash
- **Depends on:** Functional interpretation of conch-shell use
- **Notes:** Proposes acoustic signaling as a more effective community-level communication mechanism than visual signaling.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; logical argument but no direct evidence for how conch shells were actually used.
- **Assessment:** Plausible functional interpretation consistent with the modeling results.

## Claim 10

- **Statement:** About 200 additional great houses dot the "greater Chaco landscape" (GCL) beyond Chaco Canyon, over an area covering approximately 60,000 square miles. Out of more than 200 known GCL great houses, only about 30 have been fully explored and mapped and even fewer have been excavated.
- **Page:** 778-779
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Kantner and Mahoney 2000; Kantner and Kintigh 2006; Van Dyke and Heitman 2021; Reed 2018
- **Depends on:** Regional survey data
- **Notes:** The 200+ outlier great house count and 60,000 sq mi area provide scale parameters.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; standard estimate in the literature.
- **Assessment:** Establishes the regional scale of the Chaco phenomenon.

---

## Claim 11

- **Statement:** Twelve massive sandstone masonry canyon great houses, including Pueblo Bonito, have standing walls up to 8m high. Caches of unusual paraphernalia, including carved and painted birds and flowers, cylinder jars that held cacao, decorated baskets, and turquoise and shell jewellery indicate a rich body of religious practices and beliefs.
- **Page:** 778
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Lekson 2006; Crown and Hurst 2009; Heitman and Plog 2015; Mattson 2016
- **Depends on:** Excavation records
- **Notes:** Establishes the material culture context linking great houses to ritual practice and exotic goods.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; well-documented in the literature.
- **Assessment:** Standard characterization of the Chaco great house complex.

---

## Claim 12

- **Statement:** ERFs (Enigmatic Rock Features, including shrines, crescents, stone circles, basins, herraduras, zambullidas, atalayas, and cairns) in Pierre's "scion" community seem to be placed intentionally within audibility zones (7 of 8, or 87.5%), while Kin Klizhin ERFs are more randomly placed throughout the landscape (2 of 3, or 66.7%, with p = 0.462).
- **Page:** 789-790
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Statistical analysis (Table S6)
- **Depends on:** ERF location data; soundshed modelling
- **Notes:** The contrast between scion and ancestral community ERF placement may indicate different organizational principles.
- **Evidence strength:** Weak; very small sample sizes.
- **Assessment:** Intriguing pattern but sample sizes are too small for confident conclusions.
