## Claims

### Claim 1

**Statement**: "There is no theoretical or empirical support for the Handicap Principle and the time is long overdue to usher this idea into an 'honorable retirement'."
**Page**: p. 267 (abstract)
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: Comprehensive review of theoretical models (Grafen 1990a,b; Hurd 1995; Szamado 1999; Lachmann et al. 2001; Getty 1998a, 2006; Grose 2011) and empirical literature
**Depends on**: The full review argument developed across the paper
**Notes**: This is the paper's central thesis. It challenges the theoretical framework most commonly invoked to explain honest signaling, which has implications for how costly signaling theory is applied to archaeological contexts.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (supported by a detailed 24-page review of theoretical development and empirical evidence)

---

### Claim 2

**Statement**: Zahavi's (1975) original handicap hypothesis proposed that "signals are reliable indicators of male quality because they are costly and reduce survival" and that "only high-quality males are able to survive the burden of these handicaps." This is the "fixed handicap hypothesis."
**Page**: pp. 272-273
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Zahavi 1975 (direct quotations, pp. 205-213)
**Depends on**: None (historical/definitional)
**Notes**: The fixed handicap model is the one most commonly invoked in archaeological applications of CST to monumental architecture: only groups that can "survive" the cost of monument construction demonstrate quality. Penn & Szamado argue this version was conclusively rejected by theoretical models.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (accurately describes and quotes the original source)

---

### Claim 3

**Statement**: "Zahavi's arguments are circular: he began arguing that costly signals evolve because they are reliable indicators of quality, and then he concluded that reliable signals evolve because they are costly."
**Page**: p. 273
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: Analysis of Zahavi 1975 text; Dawkins 1976 critique
**Depends on**: Reading of Zahavi 1975
**Notes**: This circularity critique applies to any archaeological argument that observes costly signals and infers reliability simply from the observation of cost. The mls-monuments model avoids this by deriving the signaling equilibrium from the Spence condition (differential marginal costs) rather than from the mere existence of costs.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (the logical critique is well-established; Dawkins 1976 made the same point)

---

### Claim 4

**Statement**: Zahavi (1977b) proposed a distinct second hypothesis: "males adjust the expression of their sexual displays according to their quality (condition-dependent signalling), and that they do this because low-quality males pay higher fitness costs for signalling than high-quality males (i.e. differential, marginal costs)."
**Page**: p. 277
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Zahavi 1977b (direct quotation from p. 603)
**Depends on**: None (historical/definitional)
**Notes**: This second hypothesis is fundamentally different from the handicap hypothesis. It corresponds to the Spence condition: signal reliability depends on differential marginal costs, not on absolute waste. This is the mechanism that actually works theoretically and that the mls-monuments model implements.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (accurate rendering of Zahavi 1977b)

### Claim 5

**Statement**: "Grafen's (1990a) strategic choice signalling model... is virtually identical to Spence's economic model" (Spence 1973, 1974). Spence's model "considered the practical problem that employers usually face when seeking to hire employees due to having incomplete information about an applicant's qualifications (i.e. their quality)."
**Page**: p. 282
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Spence 1973, 1974; Grafen 1990a; Riley 1979
**Depends on**: None (historical/comparative)
**Notes**: Directly relevant to the mls-monuments model, which explicitly invokes the Spence condition. Penn & Szamado confirm that the condition-dependent signaling model (which is what works theoretically) is the Spence job-market model, not the handicap model. Spence's assumption is key: "the costs of signalling are negatively correlated with productive capability" (Spence 1973, p. 358).

**Evidence strength**: Strong (well-documented parallel between the models)

### Claim 6

**Statement**: "Signalling costs paid at the equilibrium are neither sufficient nor necessary to maintain signal reliability" and "honesty can evolve through differential benefits, as well as differential costs."
**Page**: p. 267 (abstract), developed pp. 283-285
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Hurd 1995; Szamado 1999; Lachmann et al. 2001; Getty 1998a; Godfray 1991; Godfray & Johnstone 2000
**Depends on**: Formal game-theoretic models
**Notes**: This is a critical theoretical point. It means that observing high absolute costs of signaling (e.g., C=0.35 labor diversion for Chaco construction) does not by itself explain why the signals are honest. What matters is differential costs (the Spence condition) and potentially differential benefits. The mls-monuments model should ensure it relies on the differential cost mechanism rather than absolute cost.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (multiple independent theoretical demonstrations)

### Claim 7

**Statement**: "Grafen's (1990a) signalling model do not vindicate the Handicap Principle, contrary to what has been widely assumed." The model "is not as novel as often assumed because it is a model of Zahavi's (1977b) second signalling hypothesis and it is nearly identical to a previously published model in economics (Spence, 1973, 1974; Riley, 1979)."
**Page**: p. 284
**Type**: interpretive
**Evidence cited by author**: Detailed analysis of Grafen 1990a equations and assumptions; comparison with Spence 1973
**Depends on**: Claims 5 and 6
**Notes**: The practical implication for the Chaco project: when citing costly signaling theory, the model should reference the Spence/condition-dependent signaling tradition rather than the handicap principle. Grafen's model works, but it is a Spence model, not a handicap model.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (the mathematical comparison is clearly presented)

---

### Claim 8

**Statement**: "Signals need not be costly at the equilibrium to be honest not even under conflict of interest nor under the assumption of costly signalling" and "Signals need not be costlier for worse signallers."
**Page**: p. 283
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Hurd 1995; Szamado 1999; Lachmann et al. 2001; Szamado et al. 2019; Getty 1998a, 2006
**Depends on**: Formal game-theoretic proofs
**Notes**: This challenges the assumption that archaeological signals must be wasteful to be reliable. For the mls-monuments model, the implication is that what matters is the differential structure of costs and benefits, not the absolute magnitude of waste. Construction that diverts 35% of labor is costly, but the signal works because the cost-quality relationship is monotonic (higher-quality groups bear proportionally less cost), not because the cost is "wasteful."

**Evidence strength**: Strong (multiple independent proofs)

### Claim 9

**Statement**: Enquist (1985) "established the foundations of ESS signalling theory." He "differentiated between variation in signalling due to either 'performance' or 'choice' of signalling." Performance-based signals are honest "not because of their cost, but because of a causal relationship or constraint between the level of performance of the signal and the information transmitted (i.e. index signals)."
**Page**: p. 280
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Enquist 1985; Davies & Halliday 1978; Clutton-Brock & Albon 1979
**Depends on**: None (historical)
**Notes**: This distinction between performance/index signals and choice/strategic signals is relevant to the Chaco model. Monument construction may function partly as an index signal (a group physically cannot build a large great house without sufficient labor force) and partly as a strategic signal (choice of how much to invest). The model treats it as strategic choice, which is appropriate for the variable investment levels observed.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (well-established theoretical distinction)

### Claim 10

**Statement**: Veblen's (1899) conspicuous consumption theory "proposed that the upper class signal their status through the visible or public displays of goods and services, which he called 'conspicuous consumption'. Such displays provide reliable status signals, he argued, because they are costly and only the wealthy can afford to squander time and money."
**Page**: p. 276
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Veblen 1899, The Theory of the Leisure Class
**Depends on**: None (historical)
**Notes**: Veblen's framework is directly relevant to the exotic goods signaling channel at Chaco (turquoise, macaws, cacao). Penn & Szamado note that Veblen's argument parallels Zahavi's handicap hypothesis and share similar logical weaknesses. However, the empirical pattern at Chaco (restricted access to long-distance goods) is better explained by the condition-dependent model: elites signal status because acquisition costs are differentially lower for those with established exchange networks.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (accurate description of Veblen's original argument)

### Claim 11

**Statement**: "Differential costs are not necessary to enforce signal reliability, as signal reliability can be explained by differential benefits (i.e. high-quality individuals obtaining greater fitness benefits than low-quality signallers)." Differential benefit models "have been labelled as 'general handicaps' and they have been interpreted as generalizing Grafen's strategic choice model to other signalling contexts."
**Page**: p. 283
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Godfray 1991; Getty 1998a; Godfray & Johnstone 2000; Johnstone 1997; Laidre & Johnstone 2013
**Depends on**: Claim 6
**Notes**: The mls-monuments model's lambda(sigma) function, which makes signaling returns endogenous to environmental uncertainty, is structurally a differential benefit model: the benefits of monument investment (cooperation network access, crisis buffering) are greater for higher-quality groups that can sustain the investment. This may provide a complementary or alternative mechanism to the differential cost (Spence condition) mechanism.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (multiple independent theoretical demonstrations)

---

### Claim 12

**Statement**: "The Handicap Paradigm needs to be replaced with a Darwinian framework for explaining honest and dishonest signals. We need to determine how fitness costs and benefits (and their trade-offs) influence the evolution of honest (and dishonest) signals."
**Page**: p. 285
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: The entire review
**Depends on**: All previous claims
**Notes**: This recommendation aligns with the mls-monuments model's approach: the model specifies cost and benefit functions and derives equilibrium conditions from their trade-offs, rather than invoking the handicap principle. The model should explicitly frame itself as using the condition-dependent/strategic choice signaling framework rather than the handicap framework.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (logical conclusion from the reviewed evidence)

---

### Claim 13

**Statement**: The acceptance of Grafen's handicap conclusions "appears to be the result of a logical mistake called affirming the consequent." The reasoning applied has been: "If a signalling model is a handicap model, then it aims to explain honesty with signal costs. Grafen's signalling model involves costs and aims to provide a general explanation for honesty, and therefore it is a handicap model."
**Page**: p. 286
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: T. Getty, personal communication
**Depends on**: Claims 6, 7
**Notes**: This logical fallacy warning is relevant to archaeological applications: observing costly construction and inferring that costliness is the mechanism maintaining honesty is affirming the consequent. The model needs to demonstrate the specific mechanism (differential costs or benefits) rather than simply pointing to the existence of costs.

**Evidence strength**: Moderate (logical argument is sound; the attribution to "affirming the consequent" is the authors' interpretation)

### Claim 14

**Statement**: Nur & Hasson (1984) "helped to clarify Zahavi's (1977b) second hypothesis, and they proposed how it could be modelled by taking an optimality approach." They "recognized the importance of differential signal costs for maintaining signal reliability" and showed that "the evolutionary signalling optimum for male sexual signals would be expected to provide a reliable indicator of their condition."
**Page**: p. 280
**Type**: methodological
**Evidence cited by author**: Nur & Hasson 1984
**Depends on**: Zahavi 1977b
**Notes**: The optimality approach to condition-dependent signaling is the mathematical framework underlying the mls-monuments model's Layer 1 equilibrium. The key insight is that signalers optimize investment given their quality, producing a reliable quality-signal mapping.

**Evidence strength**: Strong (accurately describes the optimality modeling approach)

## Claim Type Definitions

- **Empirical**: Direct observation or measurement reported by the author
- **Interpretive**: Inference from empirical data; not directly observable
- **Speculative**: Acknowledged by the author as tentative, or basis unclear
- **Methodological**: Claim about how to conduct or interpret research

## Evidence Strength Ratings

- **Strong**: Claim is well-supported by the evidence presented
- **Moderate**: Claim is partially supported; some evidence but gaps remain
- **Weak**: Claim is poorly supported by the evidence presented
- **Assertion without evidence**: No evidence cited; claim is stated as fact without support

## Summary Assessment

Penn & Szamado 2020 provides a comprehensive critical review of the Handicap Principle that has direct implications for how the Chaco costly signaling model frames its theoretical foundation. The paper's central contribution is the clear distinction between (a) the handicap hypothesis (signals are honest because they are wasteful/costly), which is theoretically unsupported, and (b) the condition-dependent/strategic choice signaling model (signals are honest because of differential marginal costs across quality types), which is the Spence model and is theoretically sound. The mls-monuments model's Layer 1 Spence condition is on solid theoretical ground, but the project should avoid invoking the "handicap principle" framing and instead explicitly position the model within the Spence/condition-dependent tradition. The paper also establishes that differential benefits (not just differential costs) can maintain signal honesty, which is relevant to the lambda(sigma) endogenous benefit mechanism.
