# Claims Extraction: Mattson 2025

**Source:** Mattson, Hannah V. 2025. Reevaluating the Organization of Lapidary Production at Chaco Canyon. *American Antiquity* 90:90-112.

**DOI:** 10.1017/aaq.2024.59

---

## Claim 1

- **Statement:** Significant jewelry-making was occurring at Pueblo Bonito, at least on par with previously documented small-house jewelry workshops, and a portion of this production was embedded within elite households.
- **Page:** 90 (abstract)
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Systematic analysis of lapidary tools from Pueblo Bonito (AMNH and NMNH collections)
- **Depends on:** Tool identification and classification methods
- **Notes:** Challenges the prevailing model that ornament production occurred primarily at small house sites.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; based on direct artifact analysis of 758 objects.
- **Assessment:** Well-supported by the data presented. Requires reconsideration of the standard narrative about production organization.

---

## Claim 2

- **Statement:** The lapidary tool assemblage from Pueblo Bonito totals 758 objects, including 335 from the structure and 423 from the mounds. This is almost eight times the number of lapidary tools as 29SJ629 and more than three times the combined assemblages from all the Chaco Project sites.
- **Page:** 95
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Direct analysis (Table 1)
- **Depends on:** AMNH, NMNH, and UNM collections
- **Notes:** The assemblage includes: 179 lapidary abraders, 325 lapstones, 121 drills/perforators, 73 files/active abraders, 27 grooved abraders, 33 saws.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; quantitative artifact counts from systematic analysis.
- **Assessment:** The scale difference (8x vs. the known workshop site) is striking and hard to dismiss.

---

## Claim 3

- **Statement:** Early excavations at Pueblo Bonito recovered over 100,000 finished ornaments of turquoise, marine shell, jet, and shale; they were found in virtually all contexts at the site but particularly concentrated within sumptuous burials and dedicatory offerings in ritual spaces.
- **Page:** 92
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Judd 1954; Pepper 1920
- **Depends on:** Hyde Exploring Expedition and National Geographic Society Expedition records
- **Notes:** The 100,000+ ornament count is a standard figure in the Chaco literature.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; based on excavation records.
- **Assessment:** The concentration of ornaments in ritual and burial contexts is well-documented.

---

## Claim 4

- **Statement:** Statistical analysis of the relative frequency of passive and active lapidary abraders indicates no significant difference between the Pueblo Bonito and 29SJ629 assemblages (chi-squared = 0.687; p = 0.406). Production-to-population indexes are comparable: 0.18 for Pueblo Bonito and 0.21 for 29SJ629.
- **Page:** 105
- **Type:** Empirical/Methodological
- **Evidence cited by author:** Statistical comparison using Costin's production parameters; artifact counts from Akins 1997; population proxies from Heitman 2016:480
- **Depends on:** Comparable definitions of tool categories
- **Notes:** The indexes normalize lapidary tools to combined manos and metates as a population proxy.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; the chi-squared test is appropriate, but the population proxy is indirect.
- **Assessment:** Supports the claim that ornament production at Pueblo Bonito was at a scale and concentration comparable to the known workshop site, after accounting for population differences.

## Claim 5

- **Statement:** The lapidary evidence in rooms near the northern burial cluster, interpreted as the core of the primary house, contains the greatest concentration of lapidary tools. The evidence in these rooms includes "abraders of socially charged shapes (sandals); greater quantities of valuable and imported raw materials that may indicate differential access to exchange networks; and probable evidence of a more ritualized ornament production process."
- **Page:** 106
- **Type:** Empirical/Interpretive
- **Evidence cited by author:** Room-by-room spatial analysis (Figure 9); Gruner 2019; Neitzel 2003
- **Depends on:** Room provenience data and house society model (Heitman 2007, 2011, 2015; Mills 2015)
- **Notes:** Rooms near the northern cluster also contain other high-status items and restricted ritual knowledge indicators.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; spatial patterns are suggestive but provenience data from the structure is limited (only general room-level information for most objects).
- **Assessment:** The co-occurrence of lapidary tools with elite/ritual contexts supports embedded production interpretation.

## Claim 6

- **Statement:** Ornament production at Pueblo Bonito was "embedded production" in Ames' (1995:174) sense: "artisans born into noble houses 'produced elite goods, not as dependents of an elite class, but as part of their roles as elite individuals.'" High-status producers made items for their own consumption or for other elite members.
- **Page:** 106
- **Type:** Interpretive
- **Evidence cited by author:** Ames 1995:174; Costin 2005; Inomata 2001; Inomata 2007:125
- **Depends on:** Cross-cultural models of craft production organization
- **Notes:** Distinguishes embedded production from attached production (where producers are non-elite dependents).
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; interpretive framework applied to the spatial and contextual evidence.
- **Assessment:** Plausible but not uniquely supported by the evidence. The data are also consistent with a model where both elite and non-elite production occurred within Pueblo Bonito.

## Claim 7

- **Statement:** Previous models assign ornaments, particularly those made from turquoise, somewhat different roles in Chacoan political strategy. Many view the production and circulation of these items as too difficult to regulate to have been controlled directly by elites, as would be the case for prestige goods in a system of wealth finance.
- **Page:** 107
- **Type:** Interpretive (review of prior models)
- **Evidence cited by author:** Earle 2001; Hagstrum 2001; Peregrine 2001; Plog et al. 2017
- **Depends on:** Prior theoretical frameworks
- **Notes:** Mattson explicitly challenges this view by showing that production evidence exists within elite household contexts.
- **Evidence strength:** N/A (characterization of prior literature)
- **Assessment:** Accurate representation of the prior scholarly consensus.

---

## Claim 8

- **Statement:** Turquoise ornament manufacture is generally interpreted as the product of "dispersed, part-time, and independent household specialization across the small-house population, carried out at the encouragement, but not under the direct control, of elites."
- **Page:** 93
- **Type:** Interpretive (review of prior models)
- **Evidence cited by author:** Earle 2001; Hagstrum 2001; Peregrine 2001; Plog et al. 2017:293-294; Toll 2006
- **Depends on:** NPS Chaco Project findings from small house excavations
- **Notes:** This is the "corporate political strategy" model that Mattson challenges.
- **Evidence strength:** N/A (characterization of prior literature)
- **Assessment:** Standard characterization of the prevailing view.

## Claim 9

- **Statement:** At 29SJ629, two floors of one pit house and features associated with a plaza contained thousands of pieces of turquoise debris, along with lithic microdrills, ground stone lapidary abraders and files, and ornaments in various stages of manufacture. One floor alone included more than 3,000 pieces of turquoise, and one of the pits in the plaza enclosed more than 30 lapidary abrading tools and microdrills.
- **Page:** 93
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Mathien 2001; Windes 1993:307-308
- **Depends on:** NPS Chaco Project excavation records
- **Notes:** Establishes the benchmark for "workshop-scale" production at small house sites.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; based on systematic excavation.
- **Assessment:** Well-documented primary data.

---

## Claim 10

- **Statement:** 94% of the Early Bonito-phase sites in the canyon contain turquoise debris, and over 40% have beads that were broken during the drilling process.
- **Page:** 93
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Windes 1992:161
- **Depends on:** Windes' surface survey data
- **Notes:** Indicates widespread, low-level turquoise working across the canyon, not just at workshop sites.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; surface survey data subject to collection bias.
- **Assessment:** Supports the dispersed production model, though does not distinguish casual use from dedicated production.

---

## Claim 11

- **Statement:** Ornament consumption was "clearly very high" at Pueblo Bonito compared to other sites. A consumption-to-production index for Pueblo Bonito yields 131.93, compared to 21.67 for all Chaco Project sites combined (excluding Pueblo Alto), 1.34 for 29SJ629, and 365.72 for 29SJ1360.
- **Page:** 107
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Calculated from artifact counts; Akins 1997; Heitman 2016:480
- **Depends on:** Comparable counting methods across sites
- **Notes:** The 29SJ1360 high index results from a burial with a necklace of ~4,000 shale beads. The true index for Pueblo Bonito is likely significantly lower than 131.93 because unexcavated mounds likely contain many lapidary tools but few finished ornaments.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; the index is a ratio of finished ornaments to lapidary tools, and both numerator and denominator are incomplete.
- **Assessment:** Despite caveats, the data clearly show that ornament consumption was disproportionately concentrated at Pueblo Bonito.

## Claim 12

- **Statement:** Certain forms, materials, and quantities of ornaments "were required by those with higher status for their own adornment, either in life or in death, or for their ritual responsibilities. I suggest that these items may have been made by members of high-ranking households through a system of embedded production."
- **Page:** 108
- **Type:** Interpretive
- **Evidence cited by author:** Gruner 2019; Plog 2018; Watson et al. 2015
- **Depends on:** Spatial distribution of lapidary tools within elite rooms, cross-cultural models of embedded production
- **Notes:** Core conclusion of the paper.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; the inference from tool location to production organization is reasonable but indirect.
- **Assessment:** Plausible but not demonstrated. The evidence shows that elite contexts contain production tools, but does not prove that elites themselves were the producers (as opposed to producers working within elite spaces).

## Claim 13

- **Statement:** Pueblo Bonito is the largest of the 12 great houses in the canyon, with approximately 650 rooms and 35 kivas (circular, semisubterranean ritual structures). Its size, architectural complexity, large number of dedicated ceremonial spaces, high-status interments, and concentration of imported and socially valuable material goods make clear that Pueblo Bonito was an important focus of Chaco identity and practice.
- **Page:** 91
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Crown 2016; Lekson 1999, 2006, 2018; Neitzel 2003
- **Depends on:** Multiple excavation reports and architectural surveys
- **Notes:** Establishes Pueblo Bonito's preeminent position within the Chaco system.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong; consensus view.
- **Assessment:** Uncontested.

---

## Claim 14

- **Statement:** Chaco's great houses are remarkable in the quantity of jewelry and imported raw materials they contain compared to both contemporary small houses and other archaeological sites in the Ancestral Pueblo region. Finished ornaments are consistently present at small houses, with a mean of 131 per site for those excavated by the Chaco Project and UNM Field Schools in the 1930s-1940s.
- **Page:** 107
- **Type:** Empirical
- **Evidence cited by author:** Brand et al. 1937; Kluckhohn and Reiter 1939; Mathien 2001; Windes 1992
- **Depends on:** Excavation records from multiple projects
- **Notes:** The mean of 131 ornaments per small house site, combined with the 100,000+ at Pueblo Bonito, quantifies the consumption differential.
- **Evidence strength:** Moderate; comparability across projects is imperfect.
- **Assessment:** The consumption differential is real and substantial, even if precise ratios are debatable.
