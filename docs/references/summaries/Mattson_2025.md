# Summary: Mattson 2025

**Full citation:** Mattson, Hannah V. 2025. Reevaluating the Organization of Lapidary Production at Chaco Canyon. *American Antiquity* 90:90-112.

**DOI:** 10.1017/aaq.2024.59

---

## Relevance to Argument

This paper is directly relevant to the signaling model's Layer 1 (individual signaling equilibrium) and the elite status signaling evidence base. Mattson demonstrates that significant ornament production occurred at Pueblo Bonito itself, not just at small house workshop sites, and that this production was embedded within elite household contexts. This challenges the prevailing model of dispersed, non-elite-controlled production and supports the interpretation that turquoise and other ornaments functioned as elite status signals with production organized to serve signaling purposes.

For the three-layer model, the key implication is that ornament production was not simply a household-level activity redistributed to great houses through a corporate political strategy. Instead, ornament production at Pueblo Bonito appears to have been at least partly an elite activity, with high-ranking household members producing items for their own use in signaling contexts (ritual, burial, adornment). This supports the Spence condition: if elites controlled both production and consumption of signaling goods, the cost-quality relationship would be quality-dependent (elites had preferential access to raw materials and production knowledge). The concentration of 100,000+ ornaments at Pueblo Bonito, combined with production evidence within elite rooms, indicates that ornament consumption was a major component of elite status display.

## Data Presented

- Complete lapidary tool assemblage from Pueblo Bonito: 758 objects (Table 1)
- Metric data for all tool types (Table 2)
- Tool type frequencies compared across Pueblo Bonito, 29SJ629, and all Chaco Project sites (Figure 2)
- Spatial distribution of lapidary tools within Pueblo Bonito by room (Figure 9)
- Comparison of grooved abrader widths with disc bead and arrow shaft diameters (Figure 6)
- Consumption-to-production indexes for multiple sites
- Statistical comparisons (chi-squared, t-tests) between Pueblo Bonito and workshop site assemblages
- Photographs and microscopic analysis of tool use-wear

---

## Methodological Notes

- Analysis based on objects in AMNH and NMNH collections, plus UNM Chaco Stratigraphy Project materials
- Tool identification based on similarity to workshop contexts at 29SJ629 and 29SJ1360
- Use-wear analysis using digital microscope at low and high magnification
- Production parameters framework from Costin (1991, 2001, 2005): concentration, intensity, scale, context
- Population proxy: combined manos and metates count (from Akins 1997, Heitman 2016)
- Limitations acknowledged: curated assemblages are incomplete; provenience data limited for many objects; study focused on Pueblo Bonito, may not be representative of other great houses
