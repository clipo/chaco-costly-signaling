## Relevance to Argument

Bliege Bird & Smith 2005 is widely cited as the key theoretical synthesis of costly signaling theory for anthropological applications. It establishes the standard four-criteria framework for evaluating costly signals that is used throughout the archaeological literature (Quinn 2019, Kantner & Vaughn 2012). The paper's integration of signaling theory with concepts of strategic interaction and symbolic capital is relevant to the project's multi-scale model, where signaling operates simultaneously in competitive (inter-group) and cooperative (intra-network) contexts. The paper's treatment of unconditional generosity as costly signaling is directly relevant to understanding feasting and public goods provision at Chaco great houses.
