## Relevance to Argument

Glatz & Plourde 2011 is one of the few archaeological studies that explicitly applies costly signaling theory to monumental architecture and generates testable spatial and temporal predictions. The paper's argument that Hittite landscape monuments moderated ongoing territorial contests (rather than commemorating hegemony) provides a cross-cultural parallel to the mls-monuments model's Layer 2 (intergroup assessment and conflict deterrence). The methodological approach of deriving and testing CST-based predictions about monument distribution is directly relevant to the Chaco project's analytical strategy.
