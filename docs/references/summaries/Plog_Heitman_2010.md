## Core Argument

Social differentiation at Chaco Canyon began approximately 150-200 years earlier than previously believed. New AMS radiocarbon dates on burials from Room 33 at Pueblo Bonito, combined with reanalysis of Pepper's archival records, demonstrate that elite interment in this mortuary crypt began in the late ninth century (AD 780-870 for Burials 13 and 14), not after AD 1020 as the prior consensus held. The authors interpret Room 33 and adjacent rooms as a cosmological microcosm where Chacoan elites linked themselves to founders, ancestors, and sacred directions to legitimize emerging social hierarchies. They frame this through the Levi-Strauss "house society" model.

## Key Data

- **Room 33 AMS dates**: 12 radiocarbon dates (Table 1) from two laboratories. Burials 13 and 14 date to the late ninth century (combined medians AD 781 and AD 873). A third early individual (femur B) has a 2-sigma range of 676-894. Remaining burials span the 10th-12th centuries.
- **Turquoise concentration**: More than 25,000 turquoise pieces from Room 33, exceeding all other prehistoric Southwestern sites combined. Burial 14 alone had 10,000+ turquoise beads, 500+ turquoise pendants, and 3,000+ shell beads/pendants. Burial 13 had nearly 6,000 turquoise pieces.
- **Room dimensions**: Room 33 is approximately 2x2 m, with more than 30,000 cataloged objects.
- **Duration of use**: Approximately 300-400 years (late ninth century through late eleventh or possibly early twelfth century).
- **Diet**: Delta-15N values for Room 33 burials (10.0-12.4 per mil) indicate higher animal protein intake than regional averages.
- **Small house burials**: 270+ individuals documented from 30+ small houses (Akins 1986), with extensive historical looting making this an underestimate.

## Methodological Notes

The paper combines AMS radiocarbon dating with reanalysis of archival records from the Hyde Exploring Expedition (1896-1899). Pepper's unpublished field notes provided precise spatial measurements (distance from ceiling beams and walls) that enabled 3D plotting of skeletal and artifact distributions. All dates calibrated using OxCal 4.1. The authors demonstrate that Pepper's water-disturbance hypothesis for skeletal disarticulation is incorrect based on preservation of fragile materials, patterned artifact distributions, and dry sediment observations.

## Relevance to Costly Signaling Model

**Layer 1 (Individual signaling)**: The paper provides the foundational chronological evidence that elite status differentiation, a prerequisite for the Spence condition, was present from the founding of Pueblo Bonito. The concentration of turquoise (25,000+ pieces, raw material sourced 170+ km away) in elite burial contexts demonstrates quality-dependent access to the costliest signaling medium. The matrilineal continuity of burial in Room 33 over 300+ years (subsequently confirmed genetically by Kennett et al. 2017) indicates a stable signaling equilibrium, not an ephemeral phenomenon.

**Layer 2 (Intergroup assessment)**: The cosmological symbolism (directional offerings, sacred mountain posts, underworld/upper world boundary) suggests that elite authority was grounded in ritual knowledge, not just material wealth, relevant to understanding what "quality" is being signaled.

**General**: The finding that the ritual, political, and social processes generating the 11th-century expansion "had begun to unfold at least a century and a half earlier" (p. 19626) supports models where individual signaling (Layer 1) precedes and drives group-level monument signaling (Layer 2).
