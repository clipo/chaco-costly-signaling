# Summary: Quinn 2019

**Full citation**: Quinn, C. P. (2019). Costly signaling theory in archaeology. In A. M. Prentiss (Ed.), *Handbook of Evolutionary Research in Archaeology* (pp. 275-294). New York: Springer.

## Relevance to Argument

Quinn 2019 is the most comprehensive recent review of costly signaling theory (CST) applications in archaeology. It is directly relevant to the project in three ways: (1) it codifies the formal conditions for honest signaling that the three-layer model must satisfy; (2) it surveys the range of archaeological applications (monuments, feasting, prestige goods) that correspond to the project's three signaling channels; (3) it identifies the central methodological challenges that the project's formal model must address, particularly demonstrating signal honesty and distinguishing CST from alternative frameworks. Quinn also notes Safi (2015), a dissertation applying CST directly to Chaco outlier great houses.

## Data Presented

No primary data. This is a review chapter synthesizing the theoretical and empirical literature on CST in archaeology. It cites approximately 80 sources spanning biology, anthropology, and archaeology.

## Methodological Notes

Quinn proposes a four-step analytical framework for evaluating CST applications: (1) identify the proposed signal; (2) specify signaler, receiver, and signaled quality; (3) demonstrate costly, quality-dependent signals; (4) show net benefits for both parties. This framework should be used as a checklist for the project's Chaco application. Quinn also emphasizes the need for formal models that generate quantitative, testable predictions rather than verbal arguments, which directly motivates the project's approach.
