## Core Argument

Watson et al. present new AMS 14C dates for 14 scarlet macaw skeletal remains from Pueblo Bonito and four from the Mimbres region of southwestern New Mexico. The direct dating reveals that macaw procurement began by approximately A.D. 900-975, 50-150 years earlier than previously postulated by Chaco scholars. Most of the 14 sampled macaws predate the apogee of architectural expansion (A.D. 1040-1110), contradicting the standard narrative that long-distance exchange with Mesoamerica was a product of the Chaco florescence. The authors propose instead that the hierarchical sociopolitical foundation of Chacoan society was established during the initial era of great house construction (no later than the mid-ninth century) and was reinforced during the late 9th and 10th centuries by the acquisition of scarlet macaws and other cosmologically powerful agents from Mesoamerica. Sociopolitical hierarchies thus evolved over nearly two centuries before taking shape in the more visible architectural and iconographic manifestations of the Chaco florescence.

## Key Data

1. **AMS 14C dates** (Table 1, p. 8241): 14 macaws from Pueblo Bonito dated. All specimens from Room 38 (12), Room 71 (1), and Room 78 (1). 14C ages range from 945 +/- 15 to 1,140 +/- 15 BP, with calibrated 2-sigma ranges spanning A.D. 780-1155.

2. **Temporal clusters**: Early group (6 macaws, A.D. 885-990, consistent with single procurement event, chi-squared=5.59, df=5, P>0.05); Middle group (6 macaws, A.D. 970-1035, chi-squared=8.98, df=5, P>0.05); Late group (2 macaws, A.D. 1015-1155).

3. **Macaw age distribution**: Of 34 macaws from Chaco, only 1 may have reached breeding age. Nestlings/juveniles/breeding-age birds comprise 2.9% of Chaco population vs. 14.0% at Paquime (z=1.82, P<0.05, one-tailed). No breeding pens or eggshells at Pueblo Bonito.

4. **Spatial concentration**: 30 of 35 canyon macaws (86%) from Pueblo Bonito alone.

5. **Procurement distance**: Scarlet macaw range extends no further north than Gulf Coast of Tamaulipas (~1,800 km from Chaco) or Pacific coast of southern Oaxaca (~2,500 km).

6. **Mimbres dates**: Four macaws dated; three from Old Town (A.D. 1015-1155), one from Mitchell (A.D. 895-1020). Mimbres macaws overlap temporally with middle cluster of Pueblo Bonito dates.

7. **Room 33 elite burials**: Previously dated (by Plog and Heitman 2010) to no later than A.D. 775-875, establishing elite social hierarchy 200 years before the florescence.

## Relevance to Signaling Model

Watson et al. 2015 contributes to the signaling model in four ways:

1. **Chronological sequencing of signaling channels** (Layers 1 and 2): The finding that exotic goods acquisition (Layer 1, individual signaling) preceded the peak of monument construction (Layer 2, group signaling) by 50-150 years supports a bottom-up emergence model. Individual-level status signaling through costly exotic goods bootstrapped the social hierarchy that later manifested in collective monumental investment. This suggests that Layer 1 should be temporally prior to Layer 2 in the model's initialization.

2. **Cost basis for exotic goods signaling** (Layer 1): The paper establishes that macaw procurement was genuinely costly along multiple dimensions: distance (1,800+ km), specialized knowledge (aviculture), organizational capacity (long-distance expeditions), and exchange goods (turquoise and hides for macaws). The absence of local breeding means every macaw represents the full acquisition cost, not the marginal cost of local production. These costs satisfy the Spence condition requirement for quality-dependent investment.

3. **Restricted distribution and quality dependence** (Layer 1): The concentration of 86% of canyon macaws at Pueblo Bonito demonstrates highly restricted access, consistent with quality-dependent signaling where only the highest-quality signaler can afford the cost. Combined with the elite burial evidence from Room 33, this establishes that exotic goods were concentrated among individuals whose status was hereditary (matrilineal, per Kennett et al. 2017) and maintained over centuries.

4. **Cosmological dimensions of signal quality**: Scarlet macaws were not merely luxury goods but carried specific cosmological significance (directional association, ritual power). The "quality" signaled by macaw possession included not just wealth but access to cosmological knowledge and ritual authority, an important dimension for defining the quality variable in the Spence condition.

## Connections to Other Sources

- **Plog & Heitman 2010**: Room 33 burial dates providing complementary evidence for early social hierarchy (cited by Watson et al. as ref. 16).
- **Kennett et al. 2017**: Matrilineal dynasty evidence from Pueblo Bonito burials, extending the elite status signaling evidence.
- **Crown 2018**: Cacao consumption evidence providing a parallel exotic goods signaling channel.
- **Kantner & Vaughn 2012**: Long-distance exchange restriction during the Chaco era (p. 77) is consistent with Watson et al.'s finding of concentrated macaw access.
- **Lentz et al. 2021**: Both papers document the Mimbres connection, though from different perspectives (macaws vs. timber).
- **Van Dyke 2004**: Van Dyke's Late Bonito construction program (A.D. 1100-1140) overlaps with the latest Watson et al. macaw dates.
- **Wills et al. 2014**: The pre-drought decline in construction investment that Wills et al. document (p. 3) coincides with the latest macaw dates, suggesting both signaling channels were declining simultaneously.
