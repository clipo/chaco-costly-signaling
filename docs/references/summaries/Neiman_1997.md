# Summary: Neiman 1997

**Full citation**: Neiman, F. D. (1997). Conspicuous consumption as wasteful advertising: A Darwinian perspective on spatial patterns in Classic Maya terminal monument dates. In G. A. Clark & C. M. Barton (Eds.), *Rediscovering Darwin: Evolutionary theory in archaeological explanation* (pp. 267-290). Arlington, VA: Archaeological Papers of the American Anthropological Association, No. 7.

## Relevance to Argument

Neiman 1997 is the foundational application of costly signaling theory to monumental architecture in archaeology. It provides the closest structural precedent for the project's Chaco model: a formal theory of "wasteful advertising" applied to an archaeological case (Classic Maya) with quantitative spatial analysis. Three contributions are directly relevant: (1) a three-factor model of advertising intensity (quality of weakest competitor, quality variance, payoff to advertising) that generates specific predictions about when and where monument construction occurs; (2) a rigorous empirical test using spatial analysis of 69 terminal monument dates, demonstrating a strong relationship between environmental conditions (rainfall) and cessation of monument construction; (3) geostatistical methods (variogram analysis) for estimating the spatial scale of political competition (~50 km), providing both a comparative benchmark and a methodological template for the Chaco analysis.

## Data Presented

- **Terminal monument dates**: Table 15.1 provides dates, fitted values, residuals, and estimated rainfall for 69 Maya sites. Sources include Bove 1981, Morley et al. 1983, Houston (n.d.), Mathews & Willey 1991, Miller 1986, and Marcus 1976.
- **Loess-fitted trend surface**: Figure 15.3 shows expected terminal dates varying from AD 760 to 900 across the Maya Lowlands, estimated with cross-validated loess (alpha = 0.90).
- **Rainfall data**: Figure 15.4 shows mean annual rainfall contours (1000-4000 mm) across the Maya Lowlands (after Rice 1993).
- **Spatial correlations**: Standardized regression coefficients: beta_distance = 0.19, beta_rainfall = 0.45, indicating rainfall has over twice the effect of geographical distance on terminal date differences.
- **Variograms**: Figure 15.6 shows semivariance of terminal date residuals vs. distance, with peak at ~65 km and return to baseline at ~150 km.

## Methodological Notes

Neiman employs several sophisticated statistical methods: (1) loess regression with cross-validation for trend estimation; (2) permutation-based testing for statistical significance of spatial correlations; (3) variogram analysis from geostatistics for detecting spatial autocorrelation and estimating the scale of political competition. The combination of local regression (to remove large-scale trends) and variogram analysis (to characterize residual spatial structure) provides a methodological template that could be adapted for analyzing spatial patterns of construction investment at Chaco.
