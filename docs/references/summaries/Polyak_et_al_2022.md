## Relevance to Argument

This paper provides a 3400-year high-resolution paleoclimate record from a speleothem in southeastern New Mexico that characterizes the entire Pueblo period (ca. AD 740-1630) as the driest and most climatically variable interval of the Late Holocene. This long-term perspective supports the costly signaling model's environmental uncertainty parameter (sigma) by showing that the Chaco florescence occurred against a backdrop of elevated aridity and variability relative to the preceding two millennia of pluvial conditions. The finding that peak aridity markers (growth hiatus, frequent aragonite layers) coincide with the Pueblo II/Chaco florescence period strengthens the lambda(sigma) prediction.

## Data Presented

The paper presents stable isotope (delta-18O, delta-13C), mineral assemblage (calcite/aragonite), and grayscale time-series data from stalagmite HC-1, supported by 34 U-series dates and 553 stable isotope analyses. The record spans approximately 3320 years (ca. 1400 BC to AD 1960). The chronology has 2-sigma uncertainties ranging from 15 to 80 years. The speleothem is from Hidden Cave in the Guadalupe Mountains of southeastern New Mexico, approximately 200 km southeast of Chaco Canyon.

## Methodological Notes

The primary proxy (calcite-to-aragonite mineralogical transition) is well-established for cave records in arid/semi-arid regions, though the authors note that prior calcite/aragonite precipitation can complicate interpretation. The 2-sigma age uncertainties (15-80 years) limit comparison with individual dendrochronologically dated droughts but are adequate for defining multi-century climatic sub-periods. The cave site is in southeastern New Mexico (Guadalupe Mountains), which is in the eastern Mogollon cultural region, not in the Ancestral Puebloan region itself. The authors argue this is appropriate because the Guadalupe Mountains region sustained rain-fed maize agriculture, but the paleoclimate signal may differ from conditions at Chaco Canyon proper due to different moisture source regions.

## Connections to Other Sources

- **Gillreath-Brown et al. 2024**: Complementary low-frequency temperature/ENSO reconstruction. Polyak et al. provide the aridity dimension; Gillreath-Brown et al. provide the temperature/ENSO dimension. Together they document both the moisture deficit and the temperature regime shift affecting the Chaco system.
- **Kantner & Vaughn 2012**: Table 2 precipitation statistics provide higher-resolution (annual) precipitation variability data for the Chaco area specifically. Polyak et al. add the multi-millennial baseline.
- **Lentz et al. 2021**: Documents the anthropogenic component of environmental degradation. Polyak et al. establish that the Pueblo period was already climatically stressed before human landscape modification amplified vulnerability.
- **Kintigh & Ingram 2018** (reference 8 in Polyak et al.): Found no robust drought-culture statistical relationship for individual events. Polyak et al. argue the relevant signal is the long-term baseline shift.
