**Full citation:** Scarborough, Vernon L., Samantha G. Fladd, Nicholas P. Dunning, Stephen Plog, Lewis A. Owen, Christopher Carr, Kenneth B. Tankersley, Jon-Paul McCool, Adam S. Watson, Elizabeth A. Haussner, Brooke Crowley, Katelyn J. Bishop, David L. Lentz, and R. Gwinn Vivian. 2018. Water Uncertainty, Ritual Predictability and Agricultural Canals at Chaco Canyon, New Mexico. *Antiquity* 92(364):870-889.

**DOI:** 10.15184/aqy.2018.114

## Relevance to Argument

This paper provides critical evidence for the environmental uncertainty parameter (sigma) and the relationship between water management, ritual authority, and labor mobilization. Through OSL dating and geochemical sourcing of canal sediments, the authors demonstrate that Bonito Phase inhabitants developed a sophisticated, multi-source water management system that required rapid, coordinated responses to unpredictable hydrological conditions. The paper directly connects ritual leadership to water management, arguing that the centralization of leadership through ritual authority was the mechanism by which labor was deployed to maintain agricultural infrastructure under environmental uncertainty.

For the three-layer model, this paper supports several key elements: (1) environmental uncertainty was a persistent feature of Chaco Canyon's setting (sigma is high); (2) responses to uncertainty required coordinated labor (supporting the cooperation network value in Layer 3); (3) ritual authority was the mechanism through which cooperation was organized (consistent with signaling-based leadership); (4) the canal system represents a collective good whose maintenance required solving coordination problems (consistent with the Stag Hunt dynamics proposed in Layer 3).

The title's juxtaposition of "water uncertainty" with "ritual predictability" directly frames the problem as one of creating social certainty to manage environmental uncertainty, which is the core of the costly signaling framework: signaling provides reliable information that enables coordination under uncertainty.

## Methodological Notes

- OSL dating: multiple samples from canal sediments and associated deposits
- AMS radiocarbon: supplementary dating of organic material within canal deposits
- ED-XRF: energy-dispersive X-ray fluorescence spectrometry for water and sediment sourcing
- 87Sr/86Sr: strontium isotope ratios to distinguish water sources
- LiDAR: 1m resolution for canal path identification
- Sediment coring and profiling across the dune dam area (300m transect)
- Integration of new data with Gordon Vivian's 1960s-1970s canal documentation
