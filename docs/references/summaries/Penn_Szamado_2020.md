## Relevance to Argument

This paper complicates but ultimately supports the theoretical framework underlying the Chaco model, provided the model is framed correctly. It demonstrates that the "handicap principle" (signals are honest because they are wasteful) is theoretically unsupported. However, the condition-dependent signaling model (the Spence model), which is what the mls-monuments model actually implements via the Spence condition, is on solid theoretical ground. The project should avoid handicap framing and explicitly use condition-dependent/strategic choice signaling language.

## Methodological Notes

- The paper is a comprehensive review, not an original theoretical contribution. The critiques summarized have been published individually elsewhere (Hurd 1995; Szamado 1999, 2011; Getty 2006; Grose 2011; Lachmann et al. 2001). The value is the synthesis and the clear historical reconstruction of how the handicap idea gained acceptance.
- The distinction between "handicap" and "condition-dependent/strategic choice" signaling is semantic to some degree, since both involve costly signals. The practical difference is in what maintains honesty: absolute waste (handicap) versus differential marginal costs or benefits (strategic choice/Spence).
- The paper does not address archaeological applications of signaling theory. The implications for monument signaling and exotic goods signaling must be drawn by the reader.
