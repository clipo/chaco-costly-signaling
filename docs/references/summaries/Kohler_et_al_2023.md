## Relevance to Argument

This paper documents the dynamics of wealth inequality across the prehispanic Pueblo Southwest, providing essential context for understanding the social conditions under which Chacoan signaling emerged and operated. The finding that Chacoan great houses drove regional inequality, the bidirectional relationship between inequality and violence, and the observation that settlement persistence increased as farmland became scarce all bear directly on the model's predictions about when and why monument-based signaling becomes valuable. The paper also raises the possibility that Chaco operated as a tributary state with coercive elements, which would require the model to incorporate both cooperative and coercive signaling channels.

## Methodological Notes

- Gini coefficients calculated from house floor area as a proxy for household wealth. This is a standard approach but assumes house size correlates with economic inequality.
- The Village Ecodynamics Project (VEP) provides high-resolution population and settlement data for the central Mesa Verde (CMV) region, with agent-based modeling of settlement dynamics.
- The "room to walk" metric measures available unoccupied dry-farming niche, providing a direct measure of the constraint on mobility.
- The "churn" metric captures regional population growth rates exceeding |0.7| per year, indexing rapid in- or out-migration.
