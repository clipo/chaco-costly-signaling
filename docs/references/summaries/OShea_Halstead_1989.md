## Relevance to Argument

This concluding chapter synthesizes the volume's case studies into a general framework for understanding cultural responses to environmental uncertainty. Three findings are directly relevant to the Chaco signaling model: (1) the concept of "social storage" through durable valuables, which provides an alternative/complementary interpretation of exotic goods accumulation at Chaco; (2) the hierarchical structuring of buffering mechanisms across scales, which parallels the model's multi-layer design; and (3) the identification of institutional path dependence in buffering systems, where mechanisms adopted for risk management develop their own dynamics and can either drive transformational change or, when overwhelmed, produce catastrophic collapse.

## Methodological Notes

- The chapter synthesizes findings from 10 case studies spanning hunter-gatherers (Rowley-Conwy and Zvelebil), pastoralists (Legge), early farmers (O'Shea; Halstead; Minc and Smith), Bronze Age states (Wright, Redding, and Pollock), and historical economies (Forbes; Garnsey and Morris; Jongman and Dekker).
- The synthetic approach identifies cross-cutting patterns across very different societies, strengthening the generalizability of the buffering framework.
