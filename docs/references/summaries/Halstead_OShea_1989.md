## Relevance to Argument

This introductory chapter establishes the foundational analytical framework for studying cultural responses to environmental uncertainty. It is directly relevant to the Chaco signaling model in three ways: (1) it formalizes the three dimensions of variability (temporal, spatial, intensity) that the model's sigma parameter must capture; (2) it establishes the four-mechanism taxonomy (mobility, diversification, storage, exchange) that structures the model's analysis of Chaco buffering strategies; and (3) it articulates the trade-off between security and efficiency that the cost parameter C formalizes. The chapter's argument that high-level buffering mechanisms must be "embedded" in regular cultural practices to remain functional provides a key theoretical justification for interpreting ongoing great house construction as network maintenance rather than crisis response.

## Methodological Notes

- Establishes the analytical approach used throughout the volume: identify the structure of variability, predict the required buffering response, evaluate cultural practices against this prediction.
- Emphasizes that the boundary between environment and culture is scale-dependent: from the household perspective, taxation is environmental; from the state perspective, it is cultural.
- The "limiting factor" concept from ecology is applied to human societies: food availability operates on a timescale suited to cultural (rather than biological or demographic) responses.
