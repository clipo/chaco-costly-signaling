## Relevance to Argument

This paper is **peripheral** to the Chaco signaling project. It examines hunter-gatherer storage in the Late Archaic southeastern U.S. (ca. 4800-3200 cal B.P.), a different region, time period, and subsistence base. However, three theoretical contributions are relevant: (1) the relationship between storage scale/centralization and social complexity, where large-scale centralized storage facilitates elite emergence while dispersed storage promotes autonomy; (2) the methodology of quantifying storage capacity (volumetric analysis, caloric conversion) applicable to Chaco great house rooms; and (3) the pathway from surplus storage to feasting and communal gatherings, paralleling interpretations of Chaco ceremonial activity. The paper also cites Wills and Windes 1989, directly connecting its theoretical framework to the Chaco literature.

## Methodological Notes

- Volumetric capacity calculated using 3D Analyst extension for ArcGIS 10.2, accounting for individual pit morphology through three-dimensional reconstructions.
- Caloric capacity assumes 70% fill rate (remaining 30% for packing, earth fill, capping materials).
- Subsistence days calculated assuming 2,440 kcal/day consumption rate (Kelly 1995:102).
- Storage extrapolation assumes excavated areas are representative of the full feature-rich corridor at each ring.
