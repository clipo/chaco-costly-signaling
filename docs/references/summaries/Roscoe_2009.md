## Relevance to Argument

Roscoe 2009 applies social signaling theory to the organization of small-scale societies using contact-era New Guinea ethnography. The paper's central argument, that social signaling resolves conflicts of interest in modular (segmented) societies, provides the ethnographic and theoretical foundation for the mls-monuments model's Layer 2 (intergroup assessment and conflict deterrence). Roscoe's framework explains how ceremonial display, warfare signaling, and leadership forms in small-scale societies function as information-transmitting systems that reduce conflict and facilitate cooperation, which is the core mechanism the mls-monuments model formalizes for Chaco.

## Methodological Notes

- The "social signaling model" appears to be a verbal/conceptual framework applied to ethnographic data, not a formal game-theoretic model.
- The use of contact-era (i.e., early colonial) ethnographic data provides rich observational evidence but raises questions about colonial-contact effects on indigenous social systems.
- The paper's 104 citations and its citation by Kantner & Vaughn 2012 indicate it is a key reference for archaeological signaling applications.
