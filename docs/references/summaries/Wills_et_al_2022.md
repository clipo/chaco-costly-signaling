# Summary: Wills et al. 2022

**Full citation:** Wills, W. H., Paulina F. Przystupa, and Katharine Williams. 2022. Communication in the Chaco World: A Consideration of Time and Labor Mobilization. *American Antiquity* 87(3):457-486.

**DOI:** 10.1017/aaq.2022.33

---

## Relevance to Argument

This paper provides quantitative spatial data on the communication and labor mobilization network connecting Chaco great houses. Using least cost path analysis (LCA) of 58 great houses across a 3,800 km^2 study area, the authors define a 1-3 hour travel-time zone as the practical source area for construction labor. This directly constrains the spatial extent of the signaling model's Layer 3 (cooperation networks): the labor pool that could be mobilized for monument construction was geographically limited by communication efficiency. The paper also provides key data on construction scheduling constraints, showing that mortar-based wall construction was limited to July-September and competed directly with agriculture for labor, supporting the model's C = 0.35 labor diversion parameter.

The paper distinguishes between literal "signaling" (smoke, fire, light) and face-to-face communication, arguing that complex construction coordination required the latter. This distinction is important for maintaining clarity about the difference between communication in the information-transmission sense and costly signaling in the evolutionary/economic sense used in this project.

## Data Presented

- Least cost path travel times between 58 great houses (Table 1; walking, running at three speeds)
- Study area: approximately 3,800 km^2 encompassing Chaco Canyon and major tributaries
- DEM: 10m resolution lidar mosaic (UNM and BLM datasets)
- Hiking function: adapted from Tobler 1993 with running modifications
- Site data: area measurements and room counts from Schelberg 1984 and Marshall and Colleagues 1979
- Time path network maps (Figures 2-3, 5-6)
- Correspondence of modern roads and time paths (Figure 7)
- Comparison of documented Chaco roads with LCA time paths (Figure 8)

---

## Methodological Notes

- LCA uses Tobler's hiking function adapted for running (average 9.4 km/h, fast 16 km/h, elite 19.3 km/h)
- Analysis confined to 58 great houses identified by Schelberg 1984 and Marshall and Colleagues 1979
- Site dimensions based on area of actual great house, not midden or associated features
- Secondary analysis adds western outliers (Aztec, Salmon) and Chaco River sites
- Python 2.7 code available in Supplemental Text 1
- Modern roads do not correspond to LCA time paths outside the canyon floor

## Connections to Other Sources

- **Lekson 1984:** Source for construction labor estimates (20,000-192,000 person hours per stage; 32 major construction events)
- **Wills 2017:** Same first author; the 2022 paper extends the labor mobilization argument with spatial analysis
- **Vivian 1992:** Source for water content of masonry (30-40%)
- **Kantner and Vaughn 2012:** Also discusses communication networks; Wills et al. provide quantitative LCA data where K&V relied on qualitative assessment
- **Van Dyke et al. 2016, 2024:** Wills et al. argue against visual signaling as a labor mobilization mechanism; Van Dyke et al. 2024 (soundshed analysis) provides an alternative acoustic communication model
- **Scarborough et al. 2018:** Both papers discuss water management and its relationship to labor organization and environmental uncertainty
