## Relevance to Argument

This paper provides the first large-scale empirical test of whether Chaco great houses were intentionally placed for landscape visibility. The finding that great houses were systematically located in high-visibility settings while great kivas were not is directly relevant to the costly signaling framework: for signals to function, they must be observable by the intended audience (Layer 2). The paper demonstrates that great house builders consistently selected sites that maximized the visibility of their constructions across local landscapes, supporting the interpretation that great houses functioned as signals to external audiences. The contrast with great kivas (experienced primarily from within) suggests different signaling functions for different architectural forms.

## Data Presented

Total viewshed analysis of 430 sites (269 great houses, 161 great kivas) across the Chaco World, using 30m ASTER DEM data. Each site evaluated within a 4km-radius study area with 5km maximum viewable distance. K-S statistical tests applied to distributions within local visibility deciles. Regional breakdowns provided for 11 subregions (Tables 2-3). Temporal breakdowns by construction century (Figures 5-6). The dataset derives from the Chaco Social Networks database (Peeples et al. 2016).

## Methodological Notes

The modified total viewshed approach (5-cell spacing approximation) is validated against true total viewsheds with negligible differences (max 0.02). The 4km study area radius is justified by cross-cultural settlement territory data (Varien 1999; Gilpin 2003), representing the "convenience catchment" of a great house community plus a 2km buffer. The 5km maximum viewing distance is based on human visual acuity literature (Ogburn 2006). The use of deciles normalizes for topographic variation across the study area. One limitation is that the analysis addresses location choice, not the visual impact of the structures themselves (height, form, masonry quality), which would add to visibility beyond what topography alone provides.

## Connections to Other Sources

- **Mills et al. 2018**: Companion paper in the same Antiquity special section. Uses the same Chaco Social Networks database. Mills et al. address network connectivity; Dungan et al. address physical visibility.
- **Kantner & Hobgood 2016**: Analyzed viewsheds of two great houses with tower kivas, concluding they were meant to be viewed locally. Dungan et al. provide the broader context showing this is part of a system-wide pattern.
- **Van Dyke et al. 2016a**: Used cumulative viewshed analysis to argue for intervisibility networks among great houses and shrines. Dungan et al. complement this by addressing visibility from the landscape, not just intervisibility between sites.
- **Glatz & Plourde 2011**: Hittite landscape monuments similarly placed to moderate territorial contests. Cross-cultural parallel for visibility as a component of monument signaling.
- **Safi & Duff 2016**: Applied CST to a Chaco outlier great house (Largo Gap) in the Cibola region, one of the study areas in this paper.
