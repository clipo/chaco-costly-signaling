## Relevance to Argument

Schwarz 2023 extends costly signaling theory from the rise of complex societies to their collapse and regeneration, introducing cost shifting and cost masking as mechanisms that explain how signaling transforms during political crises. The Maya case study provides a comparative framework for understanding how Chaco's signaling system may have degraded during the Late Bonito period rather than collapsing instantaneously.

## Data Presented

- Seven-site comparison of fineware ceramics (Plumbate, Fine Orange, Fine Gray) as percentage of assemblages (Table 1, p. 17)
- Obsidian-to-chert ratios for Classic centers (up to 1.2 at Seibal) vs. Postclassic communities (0.05-0.19) (Table 2, derived from Schwarz 2013a and Kingsley 2014)
- Obsidian source provenance data (three principal types: El Chayal, Ixtepeque, San Juan Jilotepeque) with shifts from El Chayal-dominant (Classic) to Ixtepeque-dominant (Postclassic) (Table 3, from Schwarz 2013a and others)
- Architectural evidence from Peten Lakes sites: Topoxte, Tayasal, Zacpeten, Quexil Islands, Ixlu, Flores Island
- Population estimates for Peten Lakes basins (Rice and Rice 1993): Sacnab-Yaxha 6800, Macanche-Salpeten 7500 at ca. AD 800 peak; Terminal Classic declines of 91% and 57%

## Methodological Notes

- The paper is a working paper (SSRN preprint), not yet peer-reviewed in its current form, though Schwarz has published extensively on the topic in peer-reviewed venues (2004, 2009, 2013a-b).
- The analysis is largely qualitative, comparing signaling dynamics across three material culture domains (architecture, ceramics, obsidian) without formal quantitative testing.
- The cost shifting and cost masking concepts are heuristic rather than formally modeled; they lack quantitative thresholds for distinguishing between the two mechanisms.
- The Maya case study focuses on the Peten Lakes region of Guatemala, a peripheral area of the Classic Maya lowlands. Results may not generalize to core areas (Tikal, Calakmul).

- **Comparative value**: Provides a collapse/regeneration comparative case for the Chaco model. The Maya trajectory (gradual degradation through cost shifting, eventual regeneration) contrasts with the Chaco trajectory (more abrupt abandonment without local regeneration).
- **Abrams 1994**: Key source for construction energetics cited by Schwarz; relevant to Chaco labor cost parameterization
- **Van Dyke 2004**: Chaco's Late Bonito McElmo-style great houses show cost shifting parallels (illusory features, reduced scale)
