## Overview

[UNVERIFIED: Summary based on abstract only. Full PDF needed.]

Dorshow & Wills present a GIS-based agricultural suitability analysis covering 3,923 km2 encompassing Chaco Canyon and the surrounding San Juan Basin. The study expands on previous analyses of a smaller area (likely Dorshow 2012) and incorporates additional variables related to hydrology, soils, and vegetation. The central finding is that the largest great houses and surrounding communities were located in extensive areas of highly suitable agricultural lands. The analysis also identifies previously unrecognized potential agricultural lands in nearby tributary drainages.

## Relevance to Costly Signaling Model

Based on the abstract, this paper is relevant to the model in two ways:

1. **Agricultural base for great house communities**: The finding that great houses correlate with high agricultural suitability supports the interpretation that great house placement was strategic with respect to productive landscapes. This is consistent with the model's assumption that signaling investment requires a productive base (the base productivity parameter).

2. **Expanded farming catchments**: The identification of previously unrecognized agricultural lands in tributary drainages supports the dispersed extensive farming model proposed in Wills 2017, which provides context for the environmental uncertainty parameter sigma.

## Key Data Points

- Study area: 3,923 km2
- Period: ca. AD 850-1150 (Bonito Phase)
- Variables: hydrology, soils, vegetation (expanded set)
- Finding: Great houses located in zones of highly suitable agricultural lands
