## Relevance to Argument

Lekson 2002 provides the most systematic alignment of resource unpredictability, violence chronology, and political history for the Pueblo Southwest. The paper directly tests the Ember and Ember (1992) cross-cultural model (war predicted by resource unpredictability and socialization for fear) against southwestern archaeological data, finding strong temporal correspondence. The Middle period peace (900-1250) corresponds to both the Chaco/Aztec political system and a gap between periods of high climatic variability, supporting the model's Layer 2 prediction that monument signaling deters intergroup conflict.

- Ember and Ember (1992) standardized regression coefficients for warfare predictors (p. 611)
- Dean's (1988, 1996) periodization of high temporal variability in tree-ring data (pp. 612-613)
- LeBlanc's (1999) three-period warfare chronology (Early, Middle, Late) with Lekson's modifications (pp. 613-615)
- Figure 2: Schematic chronologies of resource unpredictability and violence (p. 615)
- Figure 3: Schematic chronologies of political centers, EP Events, and warfare (p. 621)
- Pueblo population data: ca. 3,000-5,000 people at Chaco, with small-scale irrigation at Aztec and large-scale irrigation at Paquime (p. 617)

## Methodological Notes

- This paper is explicitly an application of the Ember and Ember (1992) cross-cultural model, not a presentation of new primary data. All empirical data are drawn from published syntheses (Dean, LeBlanc, Turner and Turner).
- The comparison between resource unpredictability and warfare chronologies involves different methodologies with different temporal resolutions: dendroclimatology (annual) vs. archaeological violence evidence (typically multi-decadal). Temporal mismatches of 50-100 years should be expected.
- Lekson acknowledges the imprecision: "While not precise, the correspondences of LeBlanc's periods of Early period raiding, Middle period peace, and Late period warfare to Dean's periods of resource unpredictability are striking" (p. 616).
- The sequential capitals thesis (Chaco-Aztec-Paquime) is from Lekson 1999 and remains contested. The Chaco-Aztec connection is broadly accepted; the Paquime link is more controversial.
- The paper does not use the term "costly signaling" and does not engage with CST literature. Its relevance to the signaling model is through its empirical data on violence and climate chronologies rather than through theoretical alignment.
