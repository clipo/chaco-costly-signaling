## Relevance to Argument

This paper provides the low-frequency paleoclimate context essential for parameterizing environmental uncertainty (sigma) in the model. The key finding that the Chaco florescence coincided with El Nino-dominated conditions, and the system's decline with a shift to La Nina-like conditions, establishes that sigma changed not just in magnitude but in kind during the late 1200s. The paper's framework (low-frequency baseline plus high-frequency variation) suggests that sigma should be decomposed into a baseline state and perturbations from that state, rather than treated as a single parameter.

## Data Presented

- **Figure 1**: Low-frequency July temperature anomaly reconstruction for SWUS, 3000 BC to present. Loess smoothing (alpha = 0.15) with standard error band.
- **Figure 2**: Comparison of SWUS temperature (Gillreath-Brown et al. 2024) with Northern Hemisphere (Moberg et al. 2005), AD 133-1900.
- **Figure 3**: ENSO Index calculated as difference between the two reconstructions. Positive = La Nina-like (warm/dry in SWUS); negative = El Nino-like (cool/wet).
- **Figures 4-7**: Spread of maize through time and latitude, with regression analyses.
- **Key values**: ENSO Index most El Nino-like in AD 1000s; shift to La Nina-like around AD 1200; temperature minimum around AD 400; marked cooling in AD 900s.

## Methodological Notes

- Temperature reconstruction uses modern analog technique (MAT) on fossil pollen assemblages, which captures low-frequency (multi-decadal to centennial) variability that tree-ring records miss.
- The ENSO Index is conceptual: it uses the SWUS-NH temperature difference as a proxy for ENSO mean state, based on the reasoning that ENSO affects SWUS temperature more than hemispheric means.
- Maize spread analysis uses IntCal20 calibration, median calibrated ages, and regression of latitude on date for frontier sites.
- The paper is published with open-access data on GitHub (cropDiffusionR package).
