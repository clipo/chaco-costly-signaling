## Relevance to Argument

Hull et al. 2014 provide the primary isotopic evidence for the structure of turquoise exchange networks at Chaco Canyon and across the broader Ancestral Puebloan world. The central finding for the signaling model is that contemporaneous populations within Chaco Canyon accessed fundamentally different turquoise procurement networks: Pueblo Bonito elites drew primarily from eastern (Rio Grande Rift) deposits while small-site inhabitants in Marcia's Rincon accessed exclusively western (Nevada) deposits. This divergence in procurement patterns supports the Spence condition for Layer 1 (individual status signaling): the cost of acquiring turquoise was quality-dependent because access to specific trade routes was socially mediated by group membership and status within the Chaco system.

## Key Claims

1. **Scale of Chaco turquoise**: Over 200,000 pieces recovered from the canyon, extraordinary compared to other Southwest sites. Nearest source (Cerrillos Hills) is 200+ km away. (p. 187)
2. **Isotopic sourcing method**: Hydrogen and copper isotope ratios measured by SIMS, with a comparative database of 800+ analyses from 22 resource areas across the western US and Mexico. (p. 188)
3. **Pueblo Bonito: eastern sources dominate**: 81% (13/16) of sourced artifacts linked to Rio Grande Rift deposits (Cerrillos Hills, Villa Grove, Orogrande), with only 4 from Nevada deposits. (p. 191)
4. **Marcia's Rincon small sites: exclusively western sources**: 100% (6/6) of sourced artifacts from Nevada deposits (Royston, Lone Mountain), with zero from Rio Grande Rift. (p. 191)
5. **Two distinct trade networks in one canyon**: The divergent procurement patterns indicate diverse populations with different exchange connections living contemporaneously. (p. 193)
6. **Aztec mirrors Pueblo Bonito; Salmon mirrors Marcia's Rincon**: The great house/small site procurement split persists at northern San Juan Basin sites. (p. 193)
7. **Bidirectional trade**: Eastern turquoise (Villa Grove, Orogrande) found at western sites (Steve Perkins, Eagle's Watch), indicating reciprocal flows. (p. 193)
8. **Virgin Puebloan intermediaries**: Moapa Valley communities may have mediated trade between western turquoise sources and the San Juan Basin. (p. 191)

## Data Presented

- Table 1 (p. 189): Hydrogen and copper isotope ratios (average and 2-sigma) for 22 turquoise resource areas
- Table 2 (pp. 192-193): Isotope ratios, associated dates, and geological source assignments for all 74 analyzed artifacts, organized by site
- Fig. 1 (p. 188): Map of turquoise deposits, archaeological sites, and inferred trade routes
- Fig. 2 (p. 189): Bivariate plot of hydrogen vs. copper isotope ratios showing deposit discrimination
- Fig. 3 (p. 196): ESS for owner-intruder game (wrong paper reference; this figure is from Enquist & Leimar)
- 29 artifacts from Pueblo Bonito; 9 from Marcia's Rincon; 13 from Aztec Ruin; 8 from Salmon Ruin; 5 from Steve Perkins; 3 from Yamashita Y3; 7 from Eagle's Watch

## Methodological Notes

- SIMS analysis with 40 nA, -12.5 kV O- primary beam focused on ~50 micron spot (p. 190)
- Hydrogen isotopes reported as delta-D relative to V-SMOW; copper isotopes as delta-65Cu relative to NIST976 (p. 190)
- Statistical errors: 4-6 per mil for D/H, 0.4-0.5 per mil for 65Cu/63Cu (p. 190)
- Source assignment by minimum centroid distance when artifacts plot in overlapping distribution patterns (p. 190)
- 54% (42/74) of artifacts successfully assigned to geological sources; remainder could not be assigned due to database limitations (p. 191)
