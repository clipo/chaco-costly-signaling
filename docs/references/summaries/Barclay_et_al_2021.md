## Relevance to Argument

This paper provides the formal theoretical foundation for extending costly signaling from quality/ability signals to intent/stake signals. The central result, that costly helping honestly signals one's stake in another's welfare, directly supports Layer 3 of the mls-monuments model (cooperation networks and crisis buffering). The formal equilibrium condition (Inequality 6) parallels the Spence condition but derives from a helping-then-trust game structure. The finding that signaling emerges at intermediate interdependence levels generates spatial predictions about monument placement in the Chaco system.

## Data Presented

- No primary archaeological data. The paper is a formal model with ethnographic illustration.
- Martu Aboriginal women's hunting and sharing data (from Bliege Bird et al. 2012, 2018; Bliege Bird and Power 2015) used as empirical motivation and illustration.
- No quantitative model outputs (analytical results only, no simulation).

## Methodological Notes

- The model is analytical (game-theoretic), not computational. It derives equilibrium conditions rather than simulating dynamics.
- The two-stage game structure (helping then trust) is more general than traditional costly signaling models (which typically have signal then response).
- The four game types (PD, Snowdrift, Stag Hunt, Harmony) allow the framework to apply across different cooperative contexts.
- The model requires only a single two-stage interaction, not repeated interactions or direct reciprocity. This is important because it shows signaling can sustain cooperation even in one-shot encounters (e.g., between groups that interact infrequently).
- The paper does not address monumental architecture or archaeological contexts directly. The application to monuments is our inference, not the authors' stated goal.

## Connections to Other Sources

- **Depends on**: Zahavi 1975 and Grafen 1990 for signaling theory foundations; Tooby and Cosmides 2008 for welfare trade-off ratio; Roberts 2005 for cooperation and signaling; Maynard Smith and Harper 2003 for signal/cue distinction.
- **Extends**: Penn & Szamado 2020 (which reviews signaling theory broadly); Bliege Bird & Smith 2005 (which applies CST to hunting/sharing).
- **Complements**: Roscoe 2009 (social signaling in small-scale societies; provides ethnographic parallel); Glatz & Plourde 2011 (archaeological application of CST to monuments); Trigger 1990 (energy expenditure as power symbol).
- **Provides formal foundation for**: The mls-monuments model's Layer 3 (cooperation networks). The intent-signaling result and medium-interdependence prediction are directly incorporated.
- **Contrasts with**: Traditional handicap models that require signals to be wasteful. Barclay et al. show signals can be cheap under certain game structures.
