# Gap Analysis: Chaco Canyon Economy and Great Houses

**Date:** 2026-03-23
**Associated bibliography:** annotated_bibliography.md

---

## Well-Covered Topics

### Costly signaling theory (general)
Extensive theoretical literature from Zahavi through Quinn 2019. The biological foundations (Zahavi, Grafen, Spence) and anthropological applications (Bliege Bird & Smith, Hawkes, Quinn) are thoroughly represented. The recent critique from Penn & Számadó (2020) on trade-offs vs. pure costs is included.

### Timber procurement and transport
Guiterman et al. (2015) and English et al. (2016) provide excellent data on timber sourcing, transport distances, and chronological shifts. This is probably the best-quantified cost dimension for Chaco construction.

### Exotic goods chronology
Watson et al. (2015) on macaws, Crown (2018) and Crown & Hurst (2009) on cacao, Hull et al. (2014) on turquoise sourcing, and Plog & Heitman (2010) on burial assemblages collectively document the exotic goods inventory with reasonable precision.

### Environmental constraints
Benson et al. (2019) on carrying capacity, Dorshow & Wills (2022) on agricultural suitability, and Scarborough et al. (2018) on water management provide a solid picture of environmental parameters.

## Under-Covered Topics

### 1. Construction labor energetics specific to Chaco
**Gap:** Abrams (1994) and Erasmus (1965) provide general architectural energetics methods, but no study appears to apply rigorous person-day labor estimates specifically to Chaco great houses. The model's C = 0.35 parameter needs Chaco-specific validation, not just Mesoamerican analogs.
**Suggested searches:** "Chaco great house labor estimate person-days," "Pueblo Bonito construction energetics," Lekson's earlier work on room-count-based estimates.

### 2. Post-collapse violence evidence (osteological)
**Gap:** LeBlanc (2017) reviews Pax Chaco, and Crown & Nichols (2022) provide a synthesis of Southwest violence, but the specific osteological and taphonomic data for pre-Chaco, during-Chaco, and post-Chaco violence rates are thin in this bibliography. The model's prediction of low conflict during florescence and high conflict after collapse needs more granular empirical support.
**Suggested searches:** "skeletal trauma Chaco Canyon," "violence rates Pueblo III Mesa Verde," "bioarchaeology conflict ancestral Puebloan," Kuckelman et al. on Castle Rock Pueblo, Turner & Turner on cannibalism evidence.

### 3. Bet-hedging theory in behavioral ecology
**Gap:** The theoretical literature on bet-hedging as a formal evolutionary strategy (distinct from risk-spreading or risk-avoidance) is not represented. The model invokes bet-hedging but the bibliography lacks the foundational papers (Seger & Brockmann 1987, Starrfelt & Kokko 2012, Philippi & Seger 1989).
**Suggested searches:** "bet hedging theory evolution," "Seger Brockmann bet hedging," "Starrfelt Kokko diversified bet hedging," "conservative bet hedging risk spreading."

### 4. Outlier great house construction investment data
**Gap:** Mackey (2019, dissertation) directly tests CST on outlier great houses, but it is the only source. Systematic data on outlier construction investment relative to core great houses would test the model's prediction that investment scales with group quality. Kantner (2003) discusses variable core-periphery relationships but does not quantify construction differentials.
**Suggested searches:** "Chaco outlier great house construction investment comparison," "outlier community architecture labor," Van Dyke's earlier work on outlier variability.

### 5. Signal depreciation models (formal)
**Gap:** The concept of signal depreciation is invoked in the three-layer model but the bibliography lacks formal models of signal decay and maintenance from evolutionary biology. The depreciation rate delta = 0.10 needs theoretical justification beyond analogy.
**Suggested searches:** "signal depreciation evolutionary biology," "honest signal maintenance cost," "badge of status degradation," Számadó on signal maintenance.

### 6. Multilevel selection and the Price equation in archaeology
**Gap:** The model uses a modified Price equation to partition individual and group selection, but the bibliography does not include archaeological applications of multilevel selection or the Price equation. This matters for positioning the formal framework.
**Suggested searches:** "Price equation archaeology," "multilevel selection human groups," "cultural group selection archaeology," "Okasha multilevel selection."

### 7. Feasting archaeology at Chaco (beyond cacao)
**Gap:** Crown (2018) covers cacao/cylinder jars, but broader feasting evidence (food remains, cooking facilities, faunal assemblages at great houses vs. small houses) is not well represented. Feasting is a competing/complementary signaling channel to monuments, and the model's channel-selection predictions require understanding why monuments dominated over feasting at Chaco.
**Suggested searches:** "Chaco Canyon feasting evidence faunal," "great house small house food remains comparison," "ritual feasting Pueblo Bonito," Potter on animal ritual.

### 8. Network formation models and partner choice
**Gap:** The Layer 3 mechanism (monument investment attracts cooperative partners) invokes partner-choice models, but the bibliography lacks the formal literature on assortative partner choice and network formation from evolutionary game theory.
**Suggested searches:** "partner choice cooperation evolution," "assortative matching costly signaling," "network formation cooperation game theory," McNamara et al. on partner choice.

## Missing Topics (No Sources Found)

### A. Comparative multi-scale signaling cases
No sources were found that explicitly model signaling at both individual and group levels simultaneously in an archaeological context. The three-layer model appears to be novel in this regard. Comparative cases where both monument construction (group) and prestige goods display (individual) co-occur would be valuable.
**Suggested searches:** "multi-scale signaling archaeology," "nested signaling individual group," "prestige goods monumental architecture co-occurrence."

### B. Chaco-period PDSI data analysis at annual resolution
The CLAUDE.md references Cook et al. (2004) for PDSI data, but no paper in the bibliography specifically analyzes the Chaco-period PDSI record in relation to construction chronology or exotic goods procurement timing. This is a critical empirical test for the model's prediction that signaling investment intensifies under environmental stress.
**Suggested searches:** "PDSI Chaco Canyon construction correlation," "drought chronology great house building," "paleoclimate Bonito phase."

### C. Economic models of Chaco (formal/quantitative)
The bibliography contains qualitative economic interpretations (Wills 2017, Kantner 2003) but no formal quantitative economic models of Chaco resource flows, trade, or labor allocation. Agent-based models or input-output models of the Chaco economy would provide a stronger empirical baseline for the signaling model.
**Suggested searches:** "agent-based model Chaco Canyon," "quantitative model Chaco economy," "formal model Puebloan exchange."
